Read this first

Two of your assumptions are backwards, and acting on them would cost you the best study material you have.

You said you assume SAT and ACT are harder than the GRE. It is the other way around, and not by a little. The GRE is the hardest vocabulary test in this group — prep companies publish 1,000+ word lists for it precisely because the words are deliberately obscure. Meanwhile the SAT removed its dedicated vocabulary questions in 2016; today it tests words only in passage context. The ACT never had a dedicated vocabulary section at all.

You said you assume the AFOQT is harder than any of them. On word rarity, no — AFOQT Word Knowledge draws from the same register of educated vocabulary as the SAT and GRE, and its word difficulty is roughly comparable to the ASVAB's. What makes it brutal is the clock: 25 questions in 5 minutes. Twelve seconds per word, no context sentence, no time to reason from roots. That is why OATTS felt harder than anything else you have taken. It was not the words. It was the speed.

What this changes: GRE word lists are not too easy to bother with — they are the hardest tier available and the single best source for what you need. Do not skip them.

Why the tests feel shallow, in numbers:

  1. A 5-question quiz shows you 5 headwords + 25 options = 30 words. That matches what you observed.
  2. The bank holds 120 headwords. At bands 3–4 only, that is 80.
  3. You know roughly half of those, so the genuinely useful pool is about 40 words.
  4. At 30 new words a day you exhaust everything worth studying in under two days.
  5. Your plan needs 50 × 22 = 1,100 words. We are short by roughly 1,020.

Your actual question

Why don’t the engines just generate infinite words?

Because a generator can only vary what is parameterised. On the math subtests the content is numbers, and numbers are infinitely variable. On the knowledge subtests the content is facts, and a fact cannot be generated — only curated.

A quadratic can be re-rolled forever because x and the coefficients are just numbers. “What does perfidious mean” has exactly one instance. The only way to get a second Word Knowledge question is to add a second real word, by hand, with its full slate of four wrong answers — each of which has to be a named mistake (the opposite, the confusable, the merely related, the plausible decoy) rather than a random filler word.

That is the whole reason the bank is 120 and not 12,000. It is not a missing feature. It is authoring volume — and it is exactly the work to spend the next few days on.

What I propose building

Two tiers, roughly 1,000 words, in batches you can start studying immediately.

01

Kill band 2 from your rotation, don’t delete it

Those 40 words stay in the bank for warm-ups but come out of your daily draw. That alone doubles the density of useful words in every quiz you take, today, before a single new word is written.

02

Build band 5 — the tier that does not exist

Roughly 300 words at GRE ceiling: abstruse, obsequious, recondite, vituperate. This is the tier above OATTS, and right now you have no way to stretch into it. Highest value per word of anything on this list.

03

Deepen band 4 from 40 to ~400

This is the realistic-AFOQT tier and the one you will actually be tested at. Sourced from the GRE and pre-2016 SAT corpora, filtered to the register the Air Force actually uses.

04

Add ~300 more at band 3

Not because you need easy words, but because a 12-second clock punishes hesitation on words you half-know. Fluency at band 3 is what buys you time to think on the band 5 items.

05

Mine the confusable pairs deliberately

Half the official OATTS items we have are confusable traps — arduous/ardent, benevolent/malevolent. Every new word gets a real confusable, so the wrong answers teach the exact mistake the Air Force is testing for.
