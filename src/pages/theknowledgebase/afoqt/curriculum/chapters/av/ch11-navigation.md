# Navigation and the pilot

Navigation is one of the five pillars the Air Force's own module tree names for Aviation
Information, so it is examinable even though none of the ten sampled official items happened to
land here. The aeromedical half is short and worth every line — hypoxia and the illusions are
standard aviation-knowledge material and they are asked.

---

## Three ways to find your way

| Method | How |
|---|---|
| **Pilotage** | Looking out of the window at **landmarks** |
| **Dead reckoning** | **Computing** position from heading, airspeed, wind and elapsed time |
| **Radio navigation** | Ground transmitters and cockpit receivers |

## The navaids

| Aid | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| **VOR** | Radials to track inbound or outbound from a ground station |
| **NDB** | A simple beacon that an **ADF needle just points at** |
| **DME** | **Distance** to a ground station (slant range) |
| **GPS** | Position from satellite signal timing |
| **ILS** | A precision approach — **both** lateral and vertical guidance |

The ILS splits into two components, and the pair gets asked:

- **Localizer** — **lateral** guidance, along the runway centreline.
- **Glideslope** — **vertical** guidance, the descent path.

*Localizer = left and right. Glideslope = the slope, so up and down.*

## ⭐ True, magnetic and compass

Four different "norths", and converting between them is the one **procedure** in this chapter.

| North | What it is |
|---|---|
| **True** | The geographic pole. **What charts are drawn to.** |
| **Magnetic** | Where the compass needle actually points |
| **Compass** | What the instrument reads after the aircraft's own interference |
| **Grid** | The vertical lines of a map projection |

Two corrections connect them:

- **Variation** — the angle between **true** and **magnetic** at a given place. A property of the
  *earth*.
- **Deviation** — error from magnetic material and electrics **inside the aircraft**. A property
  of *that airframe*, which is why each one carries a small correction card by the compass.

### The TVMDC chain

> **T**rue → **V**ariation → **M**agnetic → **D**eviation → **C**ompass
> *"True Virgins Make Dull Companions."*

Going **down** the chain (chart → cockpit) you **add westerly** corrections. Going back **up**,
you subtract them. *East is least, west is best* — subtract easterly variation, add westerly.

Two chart lines built on this:

- **Isogonic line** — joins points of **equal variation**.
- **Agonic line** — the line of **zero** variation, where true and magnetic north agree.

*(Do not confuse either with an **isobar**, which joins points of equal atmospheric pressure on a
weather chart.)*

## Time and routes

- **Zulu time** is Coordinated Universal Time, used worldwide in aviation so that no flight
  crosses a time zone boundary on paper. **Local time** is deliberately avoided for planning.
- A **great circle** is the **shortest** route between two points on the earth — but its heading
  changes continuously.
- A **rhumb line** crosses every meridian at the same angle, so it holds a **constant heading**
  and is *not* the shortest route.

## The pilot as a system

### The four hypoxias

A clean confusion set — each is a plausible wrong answer for the others, so learn them by cause:

| Type | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Hypoxic** | Not enough oxygen **pressure** — you are simply too high |
| **Hypemic** | The **blood** cannot carry it — carbon monoxide poisoning, blood loss |
| **Stagnant** | The blood is not **moving** — poor circulation, high G |
| **Histotoxic** | The **cells** cannot use it — alcohol, drugs |

**Hyperventilation** is the near-twin: over-breathing flushes out carbon dioxide and produces
symptoms easily mistaken for hypoxia.

Supplemental oxygen becomes required for the crew after **30 minutes above a cabin altitude of
12,500 feet**.

### Spatial disorientation

Your inner ear is not a reliable attitude instrument. When what your body reports disagrees with
what the panel says, **the panel is right**. That rule is absolute in instrument conditions.

| Illusion | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Graveyard spiral** | After a long steady turn, correcting a descent makes you re-enter the turn |
| **Somatogravic** | Rapid **acceleration feels like a nose-up pitch**, tempting you to push down |
| **False horizon** | A sloping cloud deck or a line of lights read as the true horizon |

**Night vision:** to see a faint object at night, look **slightly to one side of it**. The centre
of the retina has no rod cells, so a dim light disappears if you stare straight at it.
