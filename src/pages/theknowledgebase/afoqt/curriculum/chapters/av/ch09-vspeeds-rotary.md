# V-speeds and rotary-wing

Pure flashcard territory, and the shortest path to points in the whole subtest. There is nothing
to reason out here — a fixed list of abbreviations with fixed meanings, asked directly. Read this
once, then drill it.

---

## The V-speeds

**The six every pilot quotes from memory:**

| Speed | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Vs0** | Stall speed, **landing configuration** — flaps and gear out |
| **Vs1** | Stall speed, **clean** |
| **Vr** | **Rotation** — raise the nosewheel on takeoff |
| **Vx** | Best **angle** of climb — most height per unit of **distance** |
| **Vy** | Best **rate** of climb — most height per unit of **time** |
| **Vne** | **Never exceed** |

> **Vx versus Vy** is asked constantly. **X for obstacle**: Vx is what clears the trees at the end
> of the runway, gaining the most height in the least *ground*. Vy gets you high fastest but uses
> more distance doing it.

**The configuration and structural limits:**

| Speed | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Va** | **Maneuvering** — below this, full control deflection stalls the wing before it breaks the airframe |
| **Vfe** | Maximum speed with **flaps extended** |
| **Vle** | Maximum speed with the gear already **extended** |
| **Vlo** | Maximum speed for **operating** the gear — raising or lowering it |
| **Vno** | Maximum **structural cruising** speed — exceed only in smooth air |

> **Vle versus Vlo:** **E** for *extended* (already down), **O** for *operating* (moving). Vlo is
> usually the **lower** of the two, because swinging the doors and legs into the airflow is harder
> on the structure than sitting there with them down.

---

## ⭐ The arcs are the same facts, drawn

The airspeed indicator is not a separate topic — it is a picture of the list above. Every arc
boundary **is** a V-speed, which is why learning them together is far more efficient than
learning nine separate things.

| Marking | Range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **White arc** | **Vs0 → Vfe** | Flap operating range |
| **Green arc** | **Vs1 → Vno** | Normal operating range |
| **Yellow arc** | **Vno → Vne** | Caution — smooth air only |
| **Red line** | at **Vne** | Never exceed |

Two consequences that get asked directly:

- The **bottom of the white arc** is the stall speed with flaps and gear **extended** (Vs0).
- The **bottom of the green arc** is the stall speed **clean** (Vs1).

The bottom of the white arc is always lower than the bottom of the green, because flaps lower
your stall speed. That is what flaps are for.

## Rotary-wing

The Air Force flies helicopters, so this appears. Only the controls and a handful of named
phenomena are ever asked.

**The controls:**

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Collective** | Changes the pitch of **all** rotor blades **together** — climb and descent |
| **Cyclic** | **Tilts the rotor disc** — forward, back and sideways movement |
| **Anti-torque pedals** | Vary **tail rotor** thrust — control heading |
| **Twist grip** | Throttle, mounted on the collective — sets engine RPM |

The **tail rotor** exists to oppose the **torque** of the main rotor. Without it the fuselage
would simply spin the other way.

**The phenomena:**

- **Autorotation** — after an engine failure, airflow *up* through the rotor keeps it turning. The
  helicopter equivalent of gliding: the rotor becomes a windmill, and the energy stored in it is
  spent to cushion the landing.
- **Translational lift** — the extra efficiency gained as the helicopter accelerates forward into
  undisturbed air.
- **Ground effect** — hovering close to the surface needs less power.
- **Dissymmetry of lift** — in forward flight the advancing blade sees more airflow than the
  retreating one, so the disc is unbalanced.
- **Retreating blade stall** — the consequence of the above, and **the thing that limits a
  helicopter's forward speed**.
- **Vortex ring state** (settling with power) — descending vertically into your own downwash and
  losing lift. **Recovery is forward cyclic** to fly out into clean air; pulling more collective
  makes it worse, which is why it catches people.
