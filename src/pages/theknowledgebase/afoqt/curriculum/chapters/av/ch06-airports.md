# Airports, markings and the pattern

**This chapter needs a clean 5/5 to test out of, on purpose.** Airfield operations is one of the
four sections the official AFOQT Knowledge Check is divided into, and it is one of the densest
scoring areas in the subtest. Two of the ten sampled official questions are here:

> *"Runway 16R is decoded as ___"* → 160 degrees, right-most runway
> *"A row of white lights on the airfield most likely indicate ___"* → runway edge

---

## ⭐ Runway numbering is a rule, not a fact

This is the best-value thing in the whole subtest, because once you have the rule you can answer
questions you have never seen.

> **A runway number is its magnetic heading with the last digit dropped.**

- Runway **27** points to magnetic **270°** — due west.
- Runway **09** points to magnetic **090°** — due east.
- Runway **16** points to magnetic **160°**.

It is rounded to the nearest ten degrees, so runway 27 covers actual headings from 265° to 274°.

Three consequences:

1. **The two ends of one runway differ by 18.** They face opposite ways — 180° apart — and the
   number is the heading over ten. Runway 09 at one end is runway 27 at the other.
2. **L, C, R distinguish parallel runways** — left, centre, right. So **16R** is heading 160°, the
   right-hand one of the parallel pair.
3. **It is measured from magnetic north**, not true north.

To go backwards: heading 040° → runway **04**. Heading 350° → runway **35**.

## The traffic pattern

Five legs, always in this order:

> **Upwind → Crosswind → Downwind → Base → Final**

| Leg | Where |
|---|---|
| **Upwind** | Parallel to the runway, in the landing direction |
| **Crosswind** | At right angles off the **departure** end |
| **Downwind** | Parallel to the runway, **opposite** the landing direction |
| **Base** | At right angles to the **approach** end, joining downwind to final |
| **Final** | Lined up with the centreline |

**All turns are to the left** unless published otherwise. The **segmented circle** on the field
shows the pattern direction in use; the **wind sock** shows wind direction and, by how far it
lifts, its strength.

And the reason it all points where it does: **you take off and land into the wind.** A headwind
lowers your groundspeed at touchdown, so you need less runway and have better control.

## Markings — the colour tells you everything

> **Runway markings are WHITE. Taxiway markings are YELLOW.**

Get that one distinction and half the questions answer themselves.

| Marking | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Runway centreline** | Dashed **white** line down the middle |
| **Taxiway centreline** | Solid **yellow** line — keep the nosewheel on it |
| **Threshold stripes** | Long white bars at the runway start; how many tells you its width |
| **Aiming point** | Two broad white rectangles ~1,000 ft in |
| **Touchdown zone** | Paired white bars marking 500-ft increments |
| **Displaced threshold** | White **arrows** leading to a bar — usable for taxi and takeoff, **not landing** |
| **Blast pad / stopway** | Yellow **chevrons** — not usable **at all** |
| **Hold-short marking** | Two solid + two dashed yellow lines — do not cross without clearance |
| **ILS critical area** | Yellow "ladder" pattern protecting the ILS signal |

> ⚠ **Arrows versus chevrons** is the pair that gets swapped. **Arrows** = you may roll on it, not
> land on it. **Chevrons** = stay off entirely.

For hold-short markings: you approach from the **solid** side and hold there. Coming the other
way, off the runway, the dashed side needs no clearance.

## ⭐ Lighting, as one memorised set

The colours are asked as a group, so learn them as a group:

| Colour | What it marks |
|---|---|
| **White** | **Runway edges** (they turn amber in the last 2,000 ft of an instrument runway) |
| **Blue** | **Taxiway edges** |
| **Green** | **Taxiway centreline** |
| **Green / red** | **Threshold** — green facing an arriving aircraft, red from the runway side |

**Beacons:**

- **White + green alternating** — a civil land airport.
- **Two quick whites + green** — a **military** airfield.

**On the aircraft itself:**

- **Position lights** — **red left wingtip, green right wingtip, white tail.** If you see green on
  your left and red on your right, the other aircraft is pointing at you.
- **Anti-collision beacon** — flashing **red**, meaning the engines are running or about to be.
- **Landing light** — forward-facing white.

**Approach guidance:**

- **VASI** — two bars. *"Red over white, you're all right. Red over red, you're dead. White over
  white, you'll fly all night."*
- **PAPI** — a single row of four. Two red and two white means on path.

## Signs

| Sign | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **White on RED** | **Mandatory** — a hold position you may not cross without clearance |
| **Yellow on BLACK** | **Location** — the taxiway you are *on* |
| **Black on YELLOW** | **Direction** — where you can *go*, with an arrow |
| **White on BLACK** | Runway distance remaining, in thousands of feet |

> Yellow **on** black tells you where you **are**. Black **on** yellow tells you where you can
> **go**. That is the pair that gets swapped.
