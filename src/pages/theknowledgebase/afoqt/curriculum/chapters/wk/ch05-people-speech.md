# Core vocabulary I - people and speech

This is the densest cluster on the real subtest: words for what someone is like, and words for
how they talk or write. It is also where the sound-alike traps bite hardest - *gregarious* and
*garrulous* both describe a person who is a lot to be around, and only one of them is about
talking. Sixty words do not fit in prose you could reread under a clock, so this chapter is a
method plus the table below. The table is what you study; the method is how you use it.

---

## The method

**1. Charge first.** Every word here reads as positive, negative, or neutral before you have
pinned down its exact sense - and on a 12.0-second clock, charge is available in about a second
where a precise definition is not. A five-option answer list usually mixes charges, so sorting by
charge alone often cuts your real choice down to two.

**2. Word parts second.** If you recognize a root or a prefix, use it to narrow the meaning
before reaching for the confusable. *Gregarious* shares its root with *gregarious herd* - the
"greg-" of a flock - which points straight at "sociable" without needing the whole gloss.

**3. The confusable last, and only to rule it OUT.** Each entry below lists the specific word
people mix it up with. That is not a second vocabulary word to learn - it's a trap to recognize
and set aside once you've placed the headword by charge and by parts.

## Before you move on

| Situation | Do this |
|---|---|
| You recognize the word's tone before its precise meaning | Sort by charge first - it is faster than definition and often narrows the choices to two |
| A word is built from a root you know | Use the root to confirm or correct the charge, then check the table's exact sense |
| An option looks like the headword but is spelled slightly differently | That is the listed confusable - it is there to be eliminated, not chosen |
| Two options seem close in meaning | One is the answer and one is `related` - close is not the same as correct; go back to the exact gloss |
