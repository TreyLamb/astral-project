# Prefixes and suffixes

Chapter 2 covers roots - the core of a word's meaning. This chapter covers the two pieces that
wrap around a root, and they do different jobs. A PREFIX changes the meaning (active to
inactive, active to reactive) without touching the part of speech. A SUFFIX changes the part of
speech (active to activate, now a verb) and often leaves the core meaning close to untouched.
Knowing which one you are looking at is itself a shortcut: a suffix alone can tell you whether
an unfamiliar headword is a noun, a verb or an adjective - often enough to eliminate an option
that is grammatically wrong for the sentence the word came from, with no definition required.

---

## 1. Negation prefixes - not every "not" is the same "not"

Several prefixes all gloss loosely as "not," and the AFOQT trades on the fact that they are not
interchangeable:

| Prefix | More precisely | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `in-` | not | **in**active, **in**complete, **in**visible |
| `dis-` | apart, away, or the reverse of | **dis**connect, **dis**perse, **dis**regard |
| `mis-` | wrongly or badly | **mis**understand, **mis**place |
| `non-` | not at all, entirely without | **non**essential, **non**fiction |
| `ob-` | in the way of, blocking | **ob**struct, **ob**stacle |
| `de-` | reverse, remove, or undo | **de**activate, **de**value |
| `an-` | without, lacking altogether | **an**archy, **an**hydrous |

`in-` is a plain negation; `dis-` implies something was connected and got pulled apart; `mis-`
does not negate at all, it means done incorrectly. All three can gloss loosely as "roughly means
not" - which is exactly why a five-option slate can carry two of them without repeating itself.

**`anti-` is the one to watch.** It means opposed to, acting against - not the same as `ante-`,
a DIRECTION prefix meaning before in time. They differ by one letter and mean unrelated things:
an antibiotic acts against bacteria; an anteroom is a room that comes before another one.
Confusing the two is the single most common error this chapter drills against.

## 2. Direction and degree prefixes

This group locates something in space, time or on a scale, rather than negating it:

| Prefix | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `pre-` | before, in front of | **pre**view, **pre**heat |
| `sub-` | under, below | **sub**marine, **sub**way |
| `inter-` | between, among | **inter**national, **inter**act |
| `ante-` | before in time | **ante**room, **ante**date |
| `circum-` | around | **circum**navigate, **circum**ference |
| `trans-` | across, beyond | **trans**port, **trans**atlantic |
| `hyper-` | over, excessive, above normal | **hyper**active, **hyper**tension |
| `hypo-` | under, deficient, below normal | **hypo**active, **hypo**thermia |
| `supra-` | above, outside the limits of | **supra**national |

**`hyper-` and `hypo-` are true opposites, and they get swapped constantly.** A hyperactive
patient has too much of something; a hypoactive one has too little. They are near-homophones on
top of being opposites - that combination is exactly why the pair earns its own line here rather
than a footnote.

## 3. Suffixes change the part of speech

A suffix is a different kind of clue: it usually leaves the core meaning close to alone and
instead tells you what KIND of word you are looking at.

| Suffix | Turns a word into | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `-able` | an adjective (capable of, able to be) | wash**able**, read**able** |
| `-ist` | a noun (one who does or believes) | art**ist**, real**ist** |
| `-escent` | an adjective (beginning to be, becoming) | adol**escent**, lumin**escent** |
| `-ify` | a verb (to make or turn into) | simpl**ify**, clar**ify** |
| `-cracy` | a noun (rule or government by) | demo**cracy**, aristo**cracy** |
| `-logy` | a noun (the study or science of) | bio**logy**, geo**logy** |

If a headword you do not recognize ends in `-ify`, you already know it is a verb before you have
defined a single letter of the root - which by itself is often enough to eliminate an option
that is the wrong part of speech for the sentence the word came from.

---

## Before you move on

| Situation | Do this |
|---|---|
| A word starts with something that glosses as "not" | Check WHICH kind of not - plain negation, reversal, and "done wrongly" are different mistakes |
| You see `anti-` or `ante-` | Confirm which one - opposed-to and before-in-time are unrelated meanings one letter apart |
| You see `hyper-` or `hypo-` | Confirm which direction - they are true opposites, not two words for "a lot" |
| The root is unfamiliar but the suffix is not | Use the suffix to fix the part of speech first - it narrows the slate even without a definition |
