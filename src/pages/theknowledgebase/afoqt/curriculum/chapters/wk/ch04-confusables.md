# Words that are mistaken for each other

Every one of the ten official OATTS Word Knowledge items names a confusable pair in its own
worked solution - "avoid confusing arduous with ardent," "don't confuse with exasperate." No
other pattern in the official material repeats ten times out of ten. That makes this chapter the
highest-yield thing in the whole subtest, and it is why its test-out gate is 5/5 rather than the
usual 4/5: a candidate who has a pair backwards is not uncertain, they are confidently wrong, and
a 4/5 gate waves that straight through.

---

## The method

**1. Both halves share a part of speech.** A pair is never a noun paired against a verb, so part
of speech will never eliminate one half of a pair for you - but it does confirm you are matching
the right word to the sentence in front of you before you commit to a meaning.

**2. Learn the ONE point of difference, not two separate definitions.** Two definitions read back
to back blur together under a clock. A single distinguishing hook - what the table below calls
the *tell* - is what survives into the test room. "Discrete has the two e's separated by the t"
is a tell. "Discrete means separate and individually distinct" is a definition, and definitions
are what get mixed up.

**3. Each pair is tested from both directions.** A pair earns two items, one built on each half,
so learning only the half you find easier leaves you exposed exactly as often as it helps you.

## Reference table

### Band 2 - pairs an educated adult already meets in print, and still gets wrong

| Pair | Tell |
|---|---|
| historic / historical | A historic day is one that mattered. A historical novel is merely set in the past. |
| continual / continuous | Continual interruptions stop and start. A continuous line never lifts off the page. |
| economic / economical | Economical is the longer word and it's the one that saves you money. Economic is about the economy itself. |
| hardy / hearty | A hearty welcome comes from the heart. A hardy plant survives the winter. |
| later / latter | Latter has two Ts for the second of two things. Later has one, and just means afterwards. |
| emigrate / immigrate | Emigrate is exit. Immigrate is in. The same move, named from the two ends. |
| raise / rise | You raise something. You rise yourself. Raise always takes an object. |
| borrow / lend | You borrow from whoever lends to you. The lender is the one who owns it. |
| accept / except | Except excludes. Accept takes it in. Both are hiding inside the word *exception*. |

### Band 3 - standard AFOQT level

| Pair | Tell |
|---|---|
| eminent / imminent | Imminent has an "i" for "immediate"; eminent has an "e" for "esteemed." |
| discreet / discrete | Discrete has the two e's separated by the t - separate letters, separate things. |
| flout / flaunt | You flout the rules and flaunt your wealth - flout defies, flaunt shows off. |
| censure / censor | A censor removes material; to censure is to condemn a person. |
| principal / principle | A principle is a rule, and both end in "-le." |
| prodigal / prodigious | Prodigal squanders; prodigious impresses. Only one of them is a compliment. |
| imply / infer | The speaker implies. The listener infers. Only one of the two is doing the talking. |
| precede / proceed | Precede carries *pre-*, before. Proceed carries *pro-*, forward. |
| persecute / prosecute | A prosecutor works in a court. A persecutor simply will not let up. |
| complement / compliment | A complement completes - the "e" is the one in *complete*. A compliment is something nice you said. |
| council / counsel | A council is a group of people. Counsel is what one of them gives you. |

### Band 4 - pairs that differ by a syllable and reverse the meaning

| Pair | Tell |
|---|---|
| ingenious / ingenuous | Ingenious hides *genius*. Ingenuous hides the "nu" of *naive*. |
| credible / credulous | A credible account is worth believing. A credulous listener believes anything. |
| tortuous / torturous | Torturous has *torture* inside it, spelled out in full. Tortuous merely winds. |
| venal / venial | A venal official takes the money. A venial fault is barely worth mentioning. |
| judicious / judicial | Judicial belongs to the judge. Judicious belongs to anyone with sense. |
| proscribe / prescribe | Pro-scribe writes it off. Pre-scribe writes it down for you. |
| deprecate / depreciate | Depreciate is about price. Deprecate is about disapproval. Only one shows up on a balance sheet. |
| abjure / adjure | Abjure is *ab-*, away from: you give it up. Adjure is *ad-*, toward: you press it on somebody else. |

## Before you move on

| Situation | Do this |
|---|---|
| You are choosing between the two halves of a pair | Recall the tell, not two competing definitions |
| A word in the slate looks like one you know, but the sentence reads oddly | Check whether it's actually the OTHER half of a pair you know |
| You've learned one half of a pair well | Go back and learn the other half - both sides get tested |
| A pair differs by only a syllable (band 4) | Read it twice before answering; these are built to be misread once |
