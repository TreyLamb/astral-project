# The method, and the twelve-second clock

Word Knowledge gives you 12.0 seconds a question - one of the tightest clocks on the whole
AFOQT. Arithmetic Reasoning gives you nearly six times as long. That gap is the whole point of
this chapter: twelve seconds is not enough time to retrieve a definition, weigh it against five
options, and commit. It is enough time for two much cheaper judgments, and the subtest is built
to reward using them.

This chapter has no words of its own. Every headword you drill on lives in the vocabulary
chapters later in this track - this one is about HOW to read whatever the bank hands you, fast.

---

## 1. Charge - the fastest signal you have

Before you can define a word, you can usually tell whether it is approving or disapproving. That
single bit - positive, negative, or neutral - is available almost on sight, well before a
definition surfaces, and it does real work: on a five-option slate, charge alone often
eliminates two or three choices before you have recalled anything precise.

> Which of the following words carries a NEGATIVE connotation: candid, generous, wasteful,
> affable, diligent?

You may not be able to define all five on sight. But *wasteful* reads as disapproving even if
its precise sense is not on the tip of your tongue - the other four are all words people use to
praise someone.

**The trap:** charge is a filter, not an answer. It will rarely narrow a slate to one option by
itself - it gets you from five choices to two or three, which is what the clock actually needs
from a first pass. The final call still has to come from meaning.

---

## 2. The reversed stem - when the question asks for the OPPOSITE

Some items ask for the word most nearly OPPOSITE in meaning to the one given, and the trap built
into that phrasing is specific: the correct MEANING of the headword is deliberately placed among
the wrong options. A reader moving at speed recognizes a word connected to the stem and picks
it - which answers "what does this word mean," not "what is its opposite."

> Which word is most nearly OPPOSITE in meaning to RELUCTANT?

*Reluctant* means unwilling or hesitant, so its opposite is something like *eager*. If the
options include a plain synonym such as *hesitant*, that is not a second correct answer sitting
there by accident - it is the item testing whether you read the last word of the stem before you
started matching.

**The trap:** recognizing that a word is *related* to the stem is not the same as recognizing
which *direction* it goes. A reversed stem punishes a candidate who has half the fact - the
topic, but not which side of it they were asked for.

---

## Before you move on

| Signal | Do this |
|---|---|
| You don't know the exact definition, but the word "feels" approving or disapproving | Use charge to cut the slate before trying to recall anything else |
| The stem asks for the word most nearly the SAME in meaning | Match on meaning as usual - charge and the word's general field both help narrow it |
| The stem asks for the word most nearly OPPOSITE in meaning | Read the last word of the stem before you scan the options - the correct meaning of the headword will be sitting there as a trap |
