# High-tier vocabulary VII - power, authority and defiance

Who holds power and how it is formally gained, exercised or given up, and who challenges, undermines or abuses it. The seventh and final high-tier chapter splits into two halves: rulers and the offices around them, and the vocabulary of resisting or betraying that authority.

## How to attack this cluster

Several pairs here share a prefix and nothing else. **Abrogate is to repeal; arrogate
is to claim without right** - opposite directions on the same "-rogate" stem. **Undermine is to
weaken; underline is to emphasise** - the two share four letters and mean nearly opposite things.
And **egalitarian is not egotistical** - one believes everyone is equal, the other believes in
oneself above everyone. When a headword shares a stem with a common word, assume you are being
tested on the difference between them, not the resemblance.

## Seen in a sentence

- The despot ruled through fear for thirty years.
- The potentate received foreign envoys in the throne room every morning.
- The demagogue whipped the crowd into a frenzy with half-truths.
- Federal troops were sent to put down the insurrection.
- He was charged with sedition after urging soldiers to disobey orders.
- The contumacious recruit refused every order from the drill instructor.
- Her iconoclastic lectures challenged every assumption in the field.
- Newspapers stoked jingoism in the weeks before the declaration of war.
- His chauvinism showed whenever a foreign team’s engineering was mentioned.
- Only the top echelon received the classified briefing.
- The new commander’s investiture took place at dawn on the parade ground.
- The king chose to abdicate rather than face a civil war.
- The government moved to abrogate the treaty within a year of signing it.
- She chose to renounce her citizenship rather than return.
- The audit uncovered years of malfeasance in the procurement office.
- His hubris convinced him the plan needed no contingency at all.
- It was an ignoble way to end an otherwise distinguished career.
- The commune was organized along strictly egalitarian lines.
- The council debated the primacy of tradition over efficiency.
- The proposal only served to polarize an already divided committee.
- Leaking the report would undermine months of careful diplomacy.
- An interregnum followed the king’s death, with no clear heir in place.
