# High-tier vocabulary II - praise, blame and scorn

Approval and condemnation at GRE level. This is the cluster where **connotation alone often answers the question** - you do not need to know that *opprobrium* means public disgrace if you can tell in half a second that it is a hostile word, because that strikes every approving option and frequently leaves exactly one.

## How to attack this cluster

Two words here sound like compliments and are not. *Officious* is not a longer way of
saying official, and *effrontery* is not a synonym for confidence. Where a word's everyday
look-alike is friendlier than the word itself, assume the word is the harsher one.

## Seen in a sentence

- The retirement speech was less a summary than a panegyric.
- They held him up as a paragon of airmanship for the whole wing.
- The debrief was meant to edify, not to embarrass anyone.
- She was solicitous of every new pilot in the squadron.
- The plucky crew flew the aircraft home on one engine.
- The decision brought opprobrium from every quarter.
- He would vituperate for ten minutes over a missed checklist item.
- The commander did not castigate him publicly, which surprised everyone.
- What began as a briefing turned into a twenty-minute harangue.
- The squadron newsletter would lampoon anyone who taxied past the line.
- He took umbrage at being asked to repeat the checklist.
- There is still a stigma attached to reporting a near miss.
- One bad report made him a pariah in his own flight.
- He called himself a philistine and refused to visit the gallery.
- The new flight chief was a martinet about uniform standards.
- He had the effrontery to correct the general mid-sentence.
- She had the temerity to question the plan in front of the wing.
- It would be churlish to refuse an apology offered that sincerely.
- The ribald toast did not survive contact with the commander’s spouse.
- An officious clerk held up the paperwork for a week over a signature.
- His sardonic reply made it clear he thought the plan was hopeless.
- Every retelling seemed designed to aggrandize his own part in it.
