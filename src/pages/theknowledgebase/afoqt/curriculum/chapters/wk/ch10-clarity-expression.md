# High-tier vocabulary IV - clarity and expression

How clearly something is expressed, how sharp a mind is, and the named forms that writing and speech take. Several of these are the words critics use about other people's work, which is exactly why they cluster on a test of educated general vocabulary.

## How to attack this cluster

Two distinctions carry most of the difficulty. **The person is perspicacious; the
writing is perspicuous.** And **an elegy mourns, a eulogy praises** - the single most-mixed-up
pair in this field, and the one worth memorising outright.

## The pairs that are actually tested

Every row in this chapter declares the word it is genuinely confused with, and that
word’s meaning is always one of the five options. Choosing it is a named mistake,
not bad luck.

| The word | Means | Not to be confused with | Which means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **esoteric** | obscure | *exoteric* | mainstream |

| **perspicacious** | shrewd | *perspicuous* | clearly expressed |
| **sagacious** | wise | *salacious* | indecent |

## Seen in a sentence

- The manual was full of esoteric terms nobody outside the shop used.
- The approval process was byzantine enough to need its own flowchart.
- Her limpid explanation made the system obvious in two minutes.
- One perspicacious question from the back exposed the whole gap.
- It was a sagacious call that saved them an hour of rework.
- His tactical acumen was never in question, only his paperwork.
- The erudite footnotes were longer than the chapter itself.
- She had catholic tastes in music, from plainsong to punk.
- The order failed to delineate who actually owned the decision.
- It took a week to disseminate the change to every squadron.
- There was a real dissonance between the brief and the actual plan.
- He winced at the solecism in the first line of the citation.
- As a raconteur he could hold the bar for an hour on one deployment.
- He flew the demonstration with real panache.
- The new hall is a pastiche of three different centuries.
- The poem reads as an elegy for a squadron that no longer exists.
- The band played something closer to a dirge than a march.
- There is no time to quibble over wording during a checklist.
- The tortuous reasoning took four pages to reach an obvious point.
- The ponderous briefing lost the room inside five minutes.
- His pungent summary of the plan ended the discussion.
- The complaints reached a crescendo just before the policy changed.
