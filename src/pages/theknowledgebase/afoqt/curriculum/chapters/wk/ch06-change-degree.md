# Core vocabulary II - change, degree and judgment

Three more clusters, sixty more words: verbs for things getting bigger or smaller, adjectives for
how much of something there is, and verbs for approving or condemning. As in the last chapter,
sixty words are a reference table, not sixty paragraphs - the table is the study surface, and the
method below is how to read it fast.

---

## The method

**1. Charge first.** Same technique as chapter 5: most of these words lean positive, negative, or
neutral before you have pinned an exact sense, and that sort is available in about a second.

**2. Word parts second.** Several of these words carry a visible Latin prefix that does real
work: *ex-* (out of, thoroughly), *de-* (away, down, thoroughly), *pro-* (forward, forth). Where
the table shows a root, it is there to be used, not memorized as trivia.

**3. The confusable last.** As before, each entry names the specific word people mistake it for -
recognize it and set it aside, rather than treating it as a second definition to learn.

One thing to watch that is specific to this chapter: several JUDGMENT verbs look like they should
be positive or negative from their *sound* alone and are the opposite. `condone` looks harsh and
means to *allow*. `laud` looks plain and means to *praise highly*. Charge only helps once you've
actually checked it against the gloss, not guessed it from how formal the word sounds.

## Before you move on

| Situation | Do this |
|---|---|
| The word describes something growing, shrinking, or ending | Look in the Change table, and check whether the charge matches the sentence's tone |
| The word describes an amount | Look in the Magnitude table - most of these are neutral, so lean on the exact gloss, not the charge |
| The word describes praising or condemning | Look in the Judgment table, and don't guess charge from how formal or harsh the word sounds - `condone` and `laud` both break that guess |
| An option looks like the headword with a couple of letters changed | That is the listed confusable, put there to be eliminated |
