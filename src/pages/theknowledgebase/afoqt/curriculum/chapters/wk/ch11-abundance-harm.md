# High-tier vocabulary V - abundance, scarcity and harm

Too much, too little, healthy and harmful. This chapter carries the bank's densest run of **near-opposite decoys** - wrong options that mean roughly the reverse of the right one.

## How to attack this cluster

A near-opposite decoy is the hardest kind to eliminate under time, because
recognising the shape of the word is not enough - you need its direction too. *Impair* sits one
letter from *repair*; *attenuate* one syllable from *accentuate*. The official solutions describe
this failure as remembering a word's axis but not which way along it the word points.

And read *noisome* twice. It has nothing whatever to do with noise.

## The pairs that are actually tested

Every row in this chapter declares the word it is genuinely confused with, and that
word’s meaning is always one of the five options. Choosing it is a named mistake,
not bad luck.

## Seen in a sentence

- There was a surfeit of volunteers and a shortage of aircraft.
- Nothing in the mess could satiate a crew coming off a twelve-hour sortie.
- The programme was cancelled for being profligate rather than for failing.
- His penurious habits survived long after the pay rise.
- An impecunious cadet, he walked everywhere on base.
- It was a fecund period for the programme, producing three new designs.
- More funding was treated as a panacea for every scheduling problem.
- He had survived every vicissitude of a thirty-year career.
- This checklist will supersede every version printed before March.
- The base sat in a salubrious spot well above the valley fog.
- A noisome smell from the fuel bay cleared the hangar.
- The most pernicious errors were the ones nobody noticed for months.
- The heat would desiccate anything left on the ramp by noon.
- Distance and weather both attenuate the signal.
- A second crew would alleviate the pressure on the night shift.
- Ice can occlude the static ports without any warning to the crew.
- Even mild dehydration will impair your night vision.
- They had to jettison the external tanks before the approach.
- A single anonymous memo did more to foment discontent than the policy did.
- Procedures ossify when nobody is willing to question them.
- Cutting training first was a myopic way to save money.
- Only exigent circumstances justify skipping the brief.
