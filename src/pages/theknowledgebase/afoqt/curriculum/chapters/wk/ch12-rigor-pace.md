# High-tier vocabulary VI - rigour, obligation and pace

How strictly a rule binds, how carefully work gets done, and how fast things move. The last of the high-tier chapters, and it closes with the two most valuable pairs in the bank.

## How to attack this cluster

**Proscribe means to forbid. Prescribe means to order.** One letter, opposite
instructions, and both are ordinary enough to appear in a real item. **Ascetic** is self-denying;
**aesthetic** is about beauty.

*Extrapolate* and *interpolate* are worth the extra minute for a second reason - they are the
same distinction Table Reading is built on. Interpolating is reading between cells you have;
extrapolating is guessing past the edge of the grid.

## Seen in a sentence

- A doctrinaire reading of the rule would have grounded the whole fleet.
- He was dogmatic about a technique the manual never mentioned.
- The scheduling conflict proved intractable for three months.
- The problem became tractable once they had the right data.
- He was punctilious about every entry in the maintenance log.
- Her fastidious preflight caught a chafed line nobody else saw.
- The quarters were spartan but perfectly serviceable.
- He kept an ascetic routine that left no room for a social life.
- The regulation proscribes any deviation without written approval.
- A perfunctory walkaround is worse than none, because it looks done.
- The dilatory response to the request cost them the whole window.
- A desultory hour of study is worth less than fifteen focused minutes.
- Twenty years of assignments made for a peripatetic family life.
- As a neophyte he asked the questions everyone else had stopped asking.
- He had a predilection for taking the longest possible taxi route.
- The puerile nickname followed him for the rest of the tour.
- The soporific drone of the cabin made the last hour dangerous.
- The fault stayed quiescent for months before it grounded anything.
- The plan was still inchoate when the deadline moved up.
- The paper chart was a cheerful anachronism in a glass cockpit.
- The report was tendentious enough that both sides rejected it.
- You cannot extrapolate a trend from two data points.
