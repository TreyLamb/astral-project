# High-tier vocabulary I - deception and truth

Words for lying, misleading, and the character of the person doing it. This is the first of six chapters pitched **above** the level the AFOQT is likely to ask, and there is a reason to start here: deception vocabulary is where English keeps its nastiest near-identical pairs, and every official Word Knowledge item we have from the Air Force names a confusable in its own worked solution.

## How to attack this cluster

On a twelve-second clock you will not have time to recall a definition for most of
these. What you will have time for is **direction**: is this word accusing someone of something,
or defending them? Sort the five options into approving and disapproving before you try to match
meanings, and you will usually be choosing between two rather than five.

## Seen in a sentence

- Asked directly who had authorised the flight, he began to prevaricate.
- He could not dissemble well enough to hide his disappointment.
- The contract was won by pure chicanery rather than by a better bid.
- The canard about the squadron disbanding circulated for a week.
- He answered the calumny with a letter naming every falsehood in it.
- He tried to inveigle her into signing before she had read it.
- It was a specious argument that collapsed under a single question.
- The argument was fallacious from its first premise onward.
- The wily instructor let them make the mistake before saying a word.
- The editor had to expurgate three pages before it could be printed.
- Her probity was never once questioned in thirty years of service.
- He was discharged for moral turpitude, not for incompetence.
- The board treated a genuine crime as though it were a peccadillo.
- It is unfair to impute a motive he has never once expressed.
- Under pressure he agreed to recant the statement he had signed.
- Her ingenuous question exposed what everyone else had tiptoed around.
- His artless account convinced them precisely because it was so unpolished.
- They wanted a disinterested party to review the incident.
- There was an implicit warning in the way he phrased the order.
- She gave him an incredulous look and asked him to repeat it.
- The flight data gave them incontrovertible proof of the sequence.
- The simulator scene had enough verisimilitude to raise his pulse.
