# The two formats, and what makes a pair wrong

Verbal Analogies asks one question in two different shapes. Learn to recognize both on sight,
because the second one is nearly three times as common as the first in real items.

This chapter has no vocabulary rows of its own - every relation you drill on lives in the four
chapters after this one. What you learn here is not a topic, it is a skill: it runs underneath
every single question this subtest ever asks you, in every chapter.

---

## 1. The two formats

**Format 1 - complete the fourth term.** You are given a full pair, then a third word, and asked
to supply the word that completes the second pair the same way.

> FINGER is to HAND as PETAL is to: (A) Stem (B) Flower (C) Leaf (D) Root (E) Thorn

**Format 2 - pick the matching pair.** You are given one pair and asked which of five *other*
pairs shares its relation. This is the primary format on the real test - it shows up roughly
three times as often as format 1.

> FINGER is to HAND as: (A) Fish : Trout (B) Petal : Flower (C) Insect : Ant (D) Wheel : Axle
> (E) Tree : Root

Both ask the exact same underlying question - "what is the relationship, and what else shares
it?" - they just hand you a different amount of the answer already filled in. Format 2 is
usually the faster read once you've named the relation, because you are matching a whole pair
against a whole pair instead of generating a bare word from memory.

---

## 2. The two checks that catch a wrong pair

Every wrong option on this subtest fails one of exactly two checks. Learn to run both,
in order, before you commit.

### Check 1 - level of association

The relation has to be the SAME KIND, not just a *related* one. A part-whole pair and a
member-category pair can look alike at a glance - both put two related nouns side by side - but
they are not interchangeable.

> ROBIN is to BIRD as: which is the same kind of relation - PETAL : FLOWER, or SPARROW : BIRD?

SPARROW : BIRD matches (a sparrow IS a bird, same as a robin IS a bird - member-category).
PETAL : FLOWER does not (a petal is a PART of a flower, not a member of a flower-category) -
close enough to tempt a fast reader, wrong kind of relation entirely.

### Check 2 - order matters

For every relation that is NOT symmetric, the direction the words run is part of the answer.
Offering the base pair's own two words in the wrong order is the single most common trap on this
subtest - it looks instantly familiar because you just read both words a second ago.

> FIRE is to SMOKE as: which is wrong on order alone - SPARK : FLAME, or SMOKE : FIRE?

SMOKE : FIRE reverses the base pair exactly - smoke does not cause fire, fire causes smoke. It
fails on order alone, independent of whether the relation type is otherwise close.

A small number of relations - synonym and antonym, covered in chapter 4 - genuinely don't care
about order (HAPPY : GLAD and GLAD : HAPPY are equally correct), so the reversed-pair trap is not
used against them. Everywhere else, order is a real signal, not a technicality.

## Before you move on

| Signal | Do this |
|---|---|
| The question gives you three words and one blank | You're in format 1 - name the relation from the first pair, then supply the matching word |
| The question gives you one pair and five candidate pairs | You're in format 2 - name the relation, then find the pair sharing it, not just a pair that LOOKS related |
| An option repeats the base pair's own two words | Check whether the order is reversed - that is the classic trap, not a bonus correct answer |
| Two options both look "related" to the base pair | Ask whether they're the SAME KIND of relation - not just whether the words feel connected |
