# The two formats, and what makes a pair wrong

Verbal Analogies asks one question in two different shapes. The second one is nearly three
times as common as the first in real items — which is exactly why every drill in this app is
built in that shape.

This is not a test of word knowledg. As you can tell by all of the examples below, none of these words are 'difficult' words. This is a test of relations among the words. How do the words relate to each other?

This chapter has no vocabulary rows of its own - every relation you drill on lives in the four
chapters after this one. What you learn here is not a topic, it is a skill: it runs underneath
every single question this subtest ever asks you, in every chapter.

---

## 1. The two formats

**Format 2 - pick the matching pair.** You are given one pair and asked which of five *other*
pairs shares its relation. This is the format every drill in this app uses.

> FINGER is to HAND as: (A) Fish : Trout (B) Petal : Flower (C) Insect : Ant (D) Wheel : Axle
> (E) Tree : Root

**Format 1 - complete the fourth term** also exists on the real test (roughly 1 in 4 items), and
you may see it on test day. It is not drilled here: building it well requires wrong answers drawn
from the exact same real-world topic as the stem (a pen's wrong answers are ink, a pencil, a
letter, a book - all pen-related, never a random unrelated word), and this app doesn't yet have
that content. Rather than hand you a version of it that plays unfair - obviously-wrong words next
to one obviously-right one - it stays off until it can be built properly. Knowing it exists is
enough: name the relation from the first pair, then supply the word that completes the second
pair the same way.

---

## 2. The check that catches a wrong pair

**Level of association: the relation has to be the SAME KIND, not just a *related* one.** A
part-whole pair and a member-category pair can look alike at a glance - both put two related nouns
side by side - but they are not interchangeable.

> ROBIN is to BIRD as: which is the same kind of relation - PETAL : FLOWER, or SPARROW : BIRD?

SPARROW : BIRD matches (a sparrow IS a bird, same as a robin IS a bird - member-category).
PETAL : FLOWER does not (a petal is a PART of a flower, not a member of a flower-category) -
close enough to tempt a fast reader, wrong kind of relation entirely.

For every relation that is NOT symmetric, the direction the words run is part of that same check.
Offering a genuine same-relation pair in the wrong order is a real trap - it looks plausible
because it uses real, correctly-related words, just backward.

> FIRE is to SMOKE as: which is wrong on order alone - SPARK : FLAME, or SMOKE : FIRE?

SMOKE : FIRE reverses the base pair - smoke does not cause fire, fire causes smoke. It fails on
order alone, independent of whether the relation type is otherwise close.

A small number of relations - two co-equal parts of one whole, like ARM and LEG - genuinely don't
care about order, so the reversed-pair trap is not used against them. Everywhere else, order is a
real signal, not a technicality.

## Before you move on

| Signal | Do this |
|---|---|
| The question gives you one pair and five candidate pairs | Name the relation, then find the pair sharing it - not just a pair that LOOKS related |
| An option repeats a real relation's own words in reverse | Check the direction before trusting it - a backward pair is not a correct one |
| Two options both look "related" to the base pair | Ask whether they're the SAME KIND of relation - not just whether the words feel connected |
