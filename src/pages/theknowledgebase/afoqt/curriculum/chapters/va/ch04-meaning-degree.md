# Synonym, antonym and degree

Three relations about meaning itself, rather than about how two things are arranged (chapter 2)
or what one does to the other (chapter 3). The whole chapter comes down to one test you can run
on any pair in about a second.

---

## The one test: "is a a weaker (or stronger) form of b?"

> - **Yes -> DEGREE.** WARM is a weaker form of HOT. Same dimension (temperature), different
>   intensity.
> - **No -> SYNONYM.** HAPPY is not a weaker form of GLAD - they are equally happy. Same
>   meaning, no intensity difference at all.

Run this test before deciding between synonym and degree. It is easy to see two words that both
"mean angry" and assume synonym without checking whether one is simply the stronger version of
the same feeling.

> ANNOYED is to ANGRY - annoyed is a weaker form of angry. Degree.
> HAPPY is to GLAD - neither is a stronger version of the other. Synonym.

---

## 1. Synonym

**a and b share the same core meaning. Order never matters.**

> FAST is to QUICK. BEGIN is to START. TERSE is to BRIEF.

Because swapping the two words changes nothing about the relation, the reversed-pair distractor
(the base pair's own words in the wrong order) is never used against a synonym pair - GLAD is to
HAPPY would be just as correct as HAPPY is to GLAD, so offering it as a "wrong" option would
secretly be a second right answer. You will not see that trap here the way you do elsewhere.

---

## 2. Antonym

**Direct opposites. Also order-independent, for the same reason as synonym.**

> HOT is to COLD. UP is to DOWN. BENEVOLENT is to MALEVOLENT.

**The natural-direction trap:** a pair only counts as a clean antonym if the OPPOSITE reads
naturally in either direction. The natural opposite of LOVE is HATE - not INDIFFERENCE, even
though indifference is also "not love." If you have to think about which word is the "more
natural" opposite, it probably is not a clean antonym pair.

---

## 3. Degree

**a and b share the same underlying dimension, at different intensities. Order MATTERS.**

> WARM is to HOT (temperature). TRICKLE is to FLOOD (water flow). IRRITATED is to FURIOUS
> (anger).

Unlike synonym and antonym, degree is never symmetric - HOT is to WARM reverses the direction of
increase, and that is a real, meaningful error, not a technicality. If the base pair is WARM is
to HOT (weak to strong), an option that flips it to HOT is to WARM is testing whether you noticed
which direction the intensity runs.

---

## Before you move on

| You're looking at... | Ask this |
|---|---|
| Two words that seem to "mean the same thing" | Can you say "a is a weaker/stronger form of b"? If yes, it's degree, not synonym |
| Two words that seem opposite | Does the reverse pairing feel just as natural? If not, it may not be a clean antonym |
| An option offers the base pair's own words, reversed | For synonym/antonym: not a real trap - order never mattered. For degree: it IS a real trap - direction matters |
