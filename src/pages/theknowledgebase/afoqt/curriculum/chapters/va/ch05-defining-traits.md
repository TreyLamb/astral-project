# What defines it

The smallest chapter in this track - one relation, two readings of it.

---

## The classic reading: a is the defining quality of b

> FIERCE is to TIGER. Fierceness is what a tiger is known for - a defining quality, not an
> incidental fact.
> SLY is to FOX. Slyness is the defining quality of a fox.

**The definitional-vs-incidental test:** is this quality part of what b IS, or just a fact that
happens to be true about b? FIERCE is to TIGER passes. STRIPED is to TIGER would fail - stripes
are a true fact about tigers, but they are not the defining thing about a tiger the way ferocity
is.

---

## The worker-domain reading: a's role is defined by setting b

> PILOT is to COCKPIT. The cockpit defines a pilot's role - it is where the skill that makes them
> a pilot is exercised, not just a place they happen to be.
> SURGEON is to HOSPITAL. A surgeon in a grocery store is still a surgeon, but the hospital is
> where that role is defined.

This is the same underlying question as the classic reading - "does b define a, or just describe
an incidental fact about a?" - applied to a role instead of a quality. **The same test applies:**
does the setting define a's role, or merely locate them? A surgeon operating outside a hospital
(in an emergency, say) is still recognizably a surgeon; the hospital is what the ROLE is
organized around, which is what makes the pairing definitional rather than incidental.

---

## Why these two count as one relation, not two

Both readings answer the exact same question - "what defines this?" - just applied to a quality
in one case and a role in the other. On the real test you will see both inside the same set of
answer choices, and a question built on the classic reading can be answered correctly by a pair
built on the worker-domain reading, because they share the identical underlying relation. Do not
treat "this one's about a job" as a signal that it must be a different kind of question.

---

## Before you move on

| You're looking at... | Ask this |
|---|---|
| An adjective (or a quality-noun) paired with a person or animal | Is this quality part of what b IS, or just a true-but-incidental fact about b? |
| A role or occupation paired with a place or subject-matter | Does that setting define the role, or would b still clearly be a-the-role without it? |
| A pair that "feels like a job description" versus one that "feels like a trait" | Both are the same relation here - do not treat them as competing answers |
