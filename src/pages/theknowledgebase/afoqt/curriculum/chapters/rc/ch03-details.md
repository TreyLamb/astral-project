# Details and paragraph function

Two question types share this chapter because they share a discipline: both require going back to
a specific, locatable part of the passage rather than reasoning from a general impression of it.

---

## 1. Detail inference - a logical step FROM a stated fact, not a restated fact

A detail-inference question is not asking you to find a sentence and repeat it back. It is asking
you to draw a conclusion that follows from a stated fact, using only what the passage actually
supports - never outside knowledge, however true that outside knowledge might be.

> Based on the passage, why might a peacetime planner favor a single efficient supply route over
> a redundant system?

The passage does not contain a sentence that directly answers this in those exact words. It states
that "the efficient single-route plan often looks better on a budget spreadsheet" in peacetime,
while redundancy's cost is the price of protecting against a risk that has not yet happened. The
correct answer connects those two stated facts into the inference the question asks for - it does
not require any information from outside the passage.

**The trap to avoid:** an answer that is true in the real world but that the passage never actually
states or implies. A detail-inference question is graded against the passage's own content, not
against general knowledge, however accurate that general knowledge happens to be.

---

## 2. Function of paragraph - WHY it is there, not WHAT it says

A function-of-paragraph question asks what job a specific paragraph does *within the passage's
argument* - not to summarize its content. "The second paragraph serves primarily to..." is asking
about purpose and structure, one level up from content.

> The second paragraph (beginning "Modern militaries have learned this lesson...") serves
> primarily to:

The correct answer here is that the paragraph "introduces a historical example that motivates the
need for redundancy discussed next" - not a restatement of the example itself. A wrong choice will
often correctly describe WHAT the paragraph says while getting WHY it is there wrong (for example,
claiming it "defines a term" that was actually defined in the paragraph before it).

**The test:** ask what would be missing from the passage's overall argument if this paragraph were
deleted. That gap is the paragraph's function.

---

## Both types reward re-reading the specific paragraph, not the whole passage

Once you have located the paragraph or sentence a question is asking about, re-read just that part
closely rather than trying to recall it from your first pass through the whole passage. Detail
inference and paragraph function are both precision questions - the answer lives in a specific,
findable place, not in a general sense of the passage's topic.

---

## Before you move on

| Signal | Do this |
|---|---|
| A detail-inference choice matches outside knowledge but isn't stated in the passage | Reject it - the answer must follow from what the passage itself says |
| A function-of-paragraph choice accurately restates the paragraph's content | Check whether it also answers WHY the paragraph is there, not just what it says |
| You're unsure of a paragraph's purpose | Ask what would be missing from the argument if that paragraph were deleted |
