# Approaching the passage

Reading Comprehension gives you roughly 10.5 seconds a question when you average across a full
passage set - not much, when a single passage runs 400 to 600 words. This chapter is about how to
spend that time well. It has no vocabulary or question types of its own; every skill it teaches is
exercised by every single RC question in every other chapter, the same way Verbal Analogies'
"the two formats" chapter underlies every analogy question without a dedicated quiz of its own.

---

## 1. Read the passage once, deliberately - not the questions first

A tempting shortcut is to skim the questions before reading the passage, hunting for exact phrases
to search for. This backfires on RC specifically, because most of the five question types here
(main idea, author agreement, detail inference, paragraph function) are not "find the sentence" -
they ask you to have actually understood the passage's structure and argument, not just located a
keyword. A candidate who skips straight to the questions usually ends up re-reading the whole
passage anyway once the first "search" comes up empty, which costs more time than reading it once,
properly, up front ever would have.

Read for two things on that first pass: what is the author's overall point, and how does each
paragraph serve it? You do not need to memorize details - the line numbers exist so you can find
them again. You do need to leave the passage with a clear sense of its shape.

---

## 2. Line numbers exist so you can return, not so you can count

Every passage prints a number down the left margin, once per line - not once every five lines, not
as a distance marker. A stem that says "as used in line 12, 'execute' most nearly means..." is
telling you exactly where to look, not asking you to count lines from the top. Go straight to that
line and read the sentence it sits in, plus the sentence before or after if the meaning still is not
clear on its own. Never try to answer a vocabulary-in-context question from memory of the passage's
general topic - the whole point of the question is that the word's specific use in that specific
sentence is what determines the correct choice.

---

## 3. The passage stays on screen - use that

A run of RC questions built around one passage keeps that passage visible for several questions in
a row before moving to a new one. This is deliberate, and it means you do not need to hold the
entire passage in memory while answering - you can and should look back at it for every single
question, including main-idea and author-agreement questions that feel like they should be
answerable "from memory." A detail you misremember with confidence is more dangerous than a detail
you have to look up.

---

## Before you move on

| Signal | Do this |
|---|---|
| You're tempted to read the questions before the passage | Don't - read the passage once, deliberately, for its overall point and structure |
| A stem references "line N" | Go directly to that line - it is a pointer, not a distance to count |
| You're unsure of an answer and the passage is still visible | Look back at it - do not answer any RC question from memory alone |
