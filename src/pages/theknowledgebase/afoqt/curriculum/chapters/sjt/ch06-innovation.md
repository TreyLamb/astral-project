# Innovation

Two judgment tests about proposing new ideas and weighing real risk. This is the thinnest
competency in the sourced material this tool was built against - see `docs/afoqt/HANDOFF.md`'s
PART 25C record for the three sources actually checked - so treat the two tests below as the
genuinely load-bearing part of this chapter, not a long list of memorized examples.

---

## 1. Process improvement - propose a genuinely better idea, with something concrete, even outside your assigned duties

The test: does the response actually bring a good idea forward, with enough concrete detail for
someone to evaluate it - rather than dropping it because it wasn't formally assigned, hiding it
because it might be rejected, or implementing it quietly without ever proposing it up the chain?

> You notice a recurring manual task could be handled by a simple tool you know how to build. The
> best response is building a rough working version on your own time and bringing it to your
> section chief with a clear case for it - not a vague verbal mention with nothing concrete to
> evaluate, and not quietly using the tool yourself without ever proposing it.

**The trap:** a past attempt at something similar that didn't work, under different conditions
years ago, is context to explain when you propose the idea again - not a permanent verdict that
means it should never be raised.

---

## 2. Calculated risk - weigh the real upside against the real downside, informed by actual detail

The test: does the response gather enough real information to genuinely weigh a risk, rather
than defaulting to caution out of habit, or approving/attempting something risky on
enthusiasm or trust alone without actually examining it?

> An untested but recommended alternate route would meet a real deadline the standard route would
> miss. The best response gets specific details, confirms basic safety and reliability, and then
> takes the route - not blind caution that never even considers whether the alternative is
> reasonable, and not acting on a colleague's secondhand assurance without checking any details.

**The trap:** rejecting a new idea just because it's unfamiliar, and approving it just because
it's exciting, are the same underlying mistake - neither one is actually evaluating the risk.

---

## Before you move on

| Signal | Do this |
|---|---|
| You have a genuinely useful idea that falls outside your assigned duties | Propose it with something concrete to evaluate - don't drop it or implement it quietly without ever raising it |
| A similar idea was tried once before and didn't work | Name what's actually changed since then - a past failure under different conditions isn't a permanent verdict |
| You're weighing an unfamiliar option against a safe, known one | Gather real, specific details before deciding - don't default to caution, and don't act on enthusiasm alone |
