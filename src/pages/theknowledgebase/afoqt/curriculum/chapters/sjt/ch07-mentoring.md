# Mentoring

Two judgment tests about developing other people, and doing it sustainably alongside your own
real workload.

---

## 1. Developmental coaching - guide someone toward the answer, and be honestly specific

The test: does the response actually help someone grow - guiding them through a problem rather
than just solving it for them, naming a specific pattern rather than staying silent about it, and
giving honest, specific feedback rather than a comforting but vague one?

> A junior team member asks you to just give them the answer instead of walking through it. The
> best response asks what they've already tried and guides them toward the answer with
> questions - not simply stating the answer, which is faster but leaves the underlying skill gap
> unaddressed for next time.

**The trap:** reassurance without any real discussion - "don't worry about it, move on" - feels
kind in the moment but wastes a genuine chance to help someone understand what to do differently.

---

## 2. Balancing mentorship with workload - protect a sustainable structure, don't silently absorb or silently drop it

The test: does the response find a genuinely sustainable way to keep supporting someone's
development, rather than either accommodating unlimited demands on your time, or abruptly cutting
someone off without an honest conversation about why?

> An informal mentoring relationship has grown from an occasional chat into a weekly commitment
> that's now cutting into your own team's work. The best response is an honest conversation that
> proposes a lighter, still-real schedule - not silently absorbing the growing cost, and not
> quietly ending the relationship without explanation.

**The trap:** giving someone unlimited time because they ask for it, or cutting them off entirely
because you're busy, are both easier in the moment than the honest conversation that actually
finds a sustainable middle.

---

## Before you move on

| Signal | Do this |
|---|---|
| Someone asks you to just solve their problem for them | Guide them toward the answer with questions - don't just hand it over or refuse to help at all |
| You notice a recurring pattern worth coaching | Name it specifically and privately - don't stay silent or raise it in public |
| A mentoring commitment has grown beyond what's sustainable | Have an honest conversation and propose a lighter schedule - don't silently absorb it or silently end it |
