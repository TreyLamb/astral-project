# Integrity and professionalism

Four judgment tests live in this chapter, and they share a common thread: doing the honest,
fair thing even when it is inconvenient, uncomfortable, or costs you something in the moment.

---

## 1. Honest reporting - report what you found, not a version shaded to look better

The test: does the response describe what actually happened, accurately, regardless of how
inconvenient that is? A report that is technically true but shaded, softened, or missing its real
cause fails this test just as much as an outright false one does.

> A flight lead deviates briefly from a planned altitude block with no safety impact and nobody
> else would have noticed. The best response reports it in the debrief exactly as it happened,
> including the real cause - not folded into an informal aside, not left out because it seemed
> minor, and *especially* not reported with the real cause quietly swapped for a more flattering one.

**The trap:** a report that mentions the event but misattributes its cause is often worse than
silence, because it actively points any follow-up in the wrong direction.

---

## 2. Conflict of interest - disclose it before it can bias anything, to someone who can judge objectively

The test: is the personal entanglement surfaced BEFORE it has a chance to affect a decision, and
to someone positioned to weigh it fairly - not managed privately by the conflicted person
themselves?

> A contracting officer's representative discovers a distant family connection to one of the
> bidding companies. The best response discloses the connection to a supervisor immediately and
> lets *them* decide whether recusal is needed - not quietly self-correcting the score to
> "compensate," which still lets the connection affect the outcome, just less visibly.

**The trap:** trying to privately manage your own bias (scoring lower to compensate, recusing
without explaining why) still keeps the decision in the hands of the one person who cannot
objectively judge it - themselves.

---

## 3. Owning mistakes - report your own error immediately, accurately, and to the person who can fix it

The test: does the response actually surface the mistake, promptly and honestly, rather than
quietly correcting it, waiting to see if it becomes a problem, or reframing its cause?

> A section lead realizes they approved a work order that skipped a required inspection step. The
> best response reports it to the chain immediately and requests reinspection - not waiting to see
> if a problem actually develops, and not quietly performing the missed step without documenting
> that it was originally skipped.

**The trap:** fixing an error quietly, without documenting that it happened, looks responsible on
the surface but hides exactly the information a future reviewer would need.

---

## 4. Fair process before accusation - route a suspicion through the process, don't act on it directly

The test: does the response gather facts through the appropriate channel before treating a
suspicion as settled - never confronting, gossiping about, or personally investigating someone
based on a hunch?

> Equipment goes missing from a supply cage, and you suspect a specific coworker based on an
> offhand comment. The best response reports the loss through the proper channel and lets a
> formal review determine what happened - not confronting the coworker directly, and not
> searching their belongings yourself.

**The trap:** even a well-intentioned personal investigation - asking around, checking someone's
things - substitutes your own suspicion for an actual fact-finding process, and risks damaging
a reputation on the strength of a hunch.

---

## Before you move on

| Signal | Do this |
|---|---|
| A report could be shaded to look better without being technically false | Report it exactly as it happened - shading is still dishonest |
| You discover a personal connection that could bias a decision | Disclose it to someone else before the decision is made, don't self-manage it privately |
| You realize you made a mistake | Report it yourself, immediately, accurately - don't wait, hide it, or misattribute the cause |
| You suspect someone of wrongdoing without proof | Route it through the proper process - don't confront, gossip, or investigate on your own |
