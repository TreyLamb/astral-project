# Leadership

Four judgment tests, all about exercising authority well - neither more nor less than the
situation actually calls for.

---

## 1. Situational authority - act at the level that is actually yours

The test: does the response make the decision that is genuinely within the responder's own
authority, without overstepping into a decision that belongs to someone senior, and without
punting a decision they are actually equipped and authorized to make?

> A flight commander recognizes that a subordinate's proposed procedure change is sound, but
> approving it is above their own delegated authority. The best response takes the idea to the
> squadron commander with an endorsement - neither approving it themselves, nor telling the
> subordinate it's simply not something they can help with.

**The trap:** correctly recognizing a decision is above your authority does not mean there is
nothing left to do - carrying a good idea forward is still your job.

---

## 2. Standards vs. morale - hold the real standard while reading the team's actual state

The test: does the response address a real lapse proportionately, informed by genuine context
(a hard recent stretch, a first offense, a reliable track record) - neither ignoring it nor
over-reacting to it?

> A reliable technician starts arriving late after two months of reduced staffing. The best
> response is a direct, private conversation that asks what's going on before judging anything -
> not silence, not immediate formal counseling, and not quietly redefining the schedule so the
> lateness disappears on paper.

**The trap:** redefining a standard to make a problem technically go away is not the same as
holding the standard - it just hides the same gap you were supposed to address.

---

## 3. Crisis triage - safety first, then mission, then paperwork

The test: under real time pressure with competing demands, does the response sequence them
correctly - an active safety issue outranks a mission-critical task, which outranks an
administrative one - and use available help efficiently rather than sequentially when both
problems are real?

> An injury occurs during an exercise at the same moment an evaluator arrives and a paperwork
> deadline looms. The best response attends to the injury first, informs the evaluator of a
> short delay, and handles the paperwork last - never delaying care for an administrative
> deadline, and never waiting on outside help for a hazard you can act on yourself right now.

**The trap:** a brief, informed delay is usually enough. Cancelling something outright, or
splitting your attention so thin that nothing gets real focus, both overcorrect.

---

## 4. Difficult personalities - address the behavior directly and privately

The test: does the response name the specific behavior to the person responsible, privately,
rather than avoiding the conversation, working around the problem silently, or confronting it in
public?

> A team member has a long-standing habit of interrupting quieter teammates, including a recent
> public incident. The best response is a private conversation naming the specific pattern - not
> a public correction in the next meeting, which would compound the very problem it's meant to fix.

**The trap:** working around a difficult behavior (calling on quieter people yourself,
reassigning someone to avoid the friction) manages the symptom but leaves the actual habit
completely unaddressed.

---

## Before you move on

| Signal | Do this |
|---|---|
| A decision is genuinely yours to make | Make it - don't escalate what you're equipped and authorized to decide |
| A decision is above your authority, but the idea behind it is good | Carry it forward with your endorsement - don't just say it's not your problem |
| A reliable performer's behavior changes | Have a direct, private conversation before judging - don't ignore it or overreact |
| Safety, mission, and paperwork compete for attention at once | Sequence safety first, mission second, paperwork last |
| Someone has a recurring difficult behavior | Address it directly and privately - not by avoidance, silent workarounds, or public confrontation |
