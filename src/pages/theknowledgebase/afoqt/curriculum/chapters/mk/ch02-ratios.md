# Ratios, proportions and rates

Four question types, one idea: **two quantities scale by the same factor.** Find the factor,
apply it, and check the units before you multiply.

Everything in this chapter is a multiplication or a division. If you find yourself adding,
you have already made the mistake this chapter exists to prevent.

---

## 1. Proportions

A proportion is two equal ratios. To solve one, find what the known pair was multiplied by,
then apply it to the other pair.

`7/9 = x/63`

The denominators went `9 → 63`, which is `× 7`. So `x = 7 × 7 = **49**`.

> **The trap:** the denominators also went *up by 54*. Adding 54 to the numerator gives 61,
> and 61 will be sitting among the answer choices. **A proportion multiplies. It never adds.**

Cross-multiplication (`7 × 63 = 9 × x`) always works and is worth using when the scale factor
is not a whole number. Just keep the pairing straight — crossing the wrong pair gives `81`,
which is also among the answer choices.

---

## 2. Splitting a total in a given ratio

The words "in the ratio 6:11" mean **17 equal parts**, not 6 or 11 of anything.

> A board 187 cm long is cut in the ratio 6:11. How long is the longer piece?

1. Parts: `6 + 11 = 17`
2. One part: `187 ÷ 17 = 11 cm`
3. Longer piece: `11 × 11 = **121 cm**`

> **The trap:** dividing 187 by 11 instead of by 17. You must divide by the number of parts,
> which is the *sum*, not by either ratio number.

Sanity check: the two pieces have to add back to the total. `66 + 121 = 187`. ✓

---

## 3. Rates, and the unit conversion hiding inside them

A rate is a ratio with units attached, and the units are where the question is won or lost.

> A printer produces 207 pages in 9 minutes. How many pages in 3 hours?

1. Rate: `207 ÷ 9 = 23 pages per **minute**`
2. The question is in **hours**. Convert first: `3 hours = 180 minutes`
3. `23 × 180 = **4140**`

> **The trap:** `23 × 3 = 69`. The rate is per minute and the question is in hours, so you
> cannot multiply them until the units match.

**Make the units match before you multiply, every time.** It is the single habit that
prevents most rate errors, and it carries straight into Arithmetic Reasoning.

### The conversions worth knowing cold

| | |
|---|---|
| 1 foot = 12 inches | 1 pound = 16 ounces |
| 1 yard = 3 feet | 1 gallon = 4 quarts |
| 1 mile = 5280 feet | 1 hour = 60 minutes |
| 1 metre = 100 cm | 1 minute = 60 seconds |
| 1 km = 1000 m | 1 week = 7 days |

Direction is the whole difficulty: going to the **smaller** unit you **multiply** (more of
them), going to the **larger** unit you **divide** (fewer of them). If the number got bigger
when the unit got bigger, you ran it backwards.

---

## 4. Scales

A map scale is just a rate: `map units × scale = real units`. Going the other way, divide.

> 1 inch = 44 miles. Two towns are 9.5 inches apart. How far apart really?
> `9.5 × 44 = **418 miles**`

> **The trap:** dropping the half. `9 × 44 = 396` is among the answer choices, and it is close enough to
> look reasonable. Half an inch is 22 miles here — a half-inch is never a rounding detail.

---

## The check that catches almost everything

Before you mark an answer, ask **"should this be bigger or smaller than what I started with?"**

- More minutes → more pages.
- A longer piece → more than half the board.
- Inches → feet → a smaller number.

Every wrong answer in this chapter fails that check. It takes about a second and it is worth
more than re-doing the arithmetic.
