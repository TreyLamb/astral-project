# Linear equations, inequalities and systems

The highest-frequency algebra topic on the test. Nothing here is conceptually hard; the marks
are lost to **sign discipline** and to **answering for the wrong variable**.

The governing rule never changes: whatever you do to one side, do to the other, until the
variable stands alone.

---

## 1. One variable, one side

`5x + 23 = 68`

1. Undo the addition: `5x = 45`
2. Undo the multiplication: `x = **9**`

Undo in reverse PEMDAS order — addition and subtraction first, multiplication and division
last. Substituting your answer back is a two-second check and catches everything.

---

## 2. Variable on both sides

Collect the variable on one side and the constants on the other. Pick the side that keeps the
coefficient positive; it is fewer chances to drop a sign.

`11x + 30 = 3x + 70`

1. Subtract `3x` from both sides: `8x + 30 = 70`
2. Subtract 30: `8x = 40`
3. Divide: `x = **5**`

> **The trap:** *adding* the two x-coefficients instead of subtracting. `11 + 3 = 14` gives
> `x ≈ 2.9`, and a rounded version of that will be on the slate. Moving a term across the
> equals sign **flips its sign** — that is the entire operation.

---

## 3. Inequalities

Everything works exactly as it does for equations, with **one** exception, and the whole
question type is built on it:

> **Multiplying or dividing by a NEGATIVE reverses the inequality sign.**
> Adding and subtracting never do.

`−4x + 30 < 6`

1. Subtract 30: `−4x < −24`
2. Divide by −4 — **and flip**: `x > **6**`

> **The trap:** `x < 6`. It is the first option on the slate and it is what you get by doing
> everything right except the one step this question exists to test.

Sanity check by substituting: does `x = 10` satisfy `−4(10) + 30 < 6`? `−10 < 6` ✓. So the
solution set is the *large* numbers, confirming `x > 6`.

Two more forms worth recognising:

- **Compound:** `−3 < 2x + 1 ≤ 9` — do the same operation to all three parts.
- **Absolute value:** `|x| < 5` means `−5 < x < 5`; `|x| > 5` means `x < −5` **or** `x > 5`.

---

## 4. Solving for one variable in terms of others

Called a *literal equation*. The letters change nothing — you are still isolating a variable.

`ax + by = c`, solve for `x`

1. Move the y-term: `ax = c − by`
2. Divide **everything** on the right by a: `x = (c − by) / a`

> **The trap:** `c/a − by`. Dividing only the first term is the classic half-finished answer.
> If you divide one term by `a`, you divide all of them.

This is the same skill as rearranging a formula, which is where it actually shows up:

| Formula | Solve for | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `A = ½bh` | h | `h = 2A/b` |
| `P = 2l + 2w` | w | `w = (P − 2l)/2` |
| `C = 2πr` | r | `r = C/(2π)` |
| `I = Prt` | r | `r = I/(Pt)` |
| `F = (9/5)C + 32` | C | `C = 5(F − 32)/9` |

---

## 5. Systems of two equations

Two methods; both are fine, and speed comes from picking the right one.

**Substitution** — best when a variable is already alone (or has coefficient 1).
**Elimination** — best otherwise. Scale one or both equations so a variable cancels when you
add or subtract.

> `4x + 3y = 47` and `2x − 5y = 3`

Multiply the second by 2 → `4x − 10y = 6`. Subtract from the first:

`13y = 41`… which is not clean, so eliminate the other way instead: multiply the first by 5
and the second by 3, then add — `26x = 244`. Also not clean. In a real item the numbers *will*
be clean; if they are not, you eliminated the harder variable. **Look at both options before
you commit.**

> ⭐ **The trap that costs the most marks on this item type:** solving the system correctly and
> then reporting `y` when the question asked for `x`. Both numbers are right there and both are
> on the answer slate. Circle the variable you were asked for before you start.

Some questions ask for `x + y` rather than either one — read for that too.

---

## Before you move on

1. Moving a term across `=` flips its sign.
2. Dividing by a negative flips an inequality; nothing else does.
3. When you divide, divide **every** term.
4. Re-read which variable the question asked for.
