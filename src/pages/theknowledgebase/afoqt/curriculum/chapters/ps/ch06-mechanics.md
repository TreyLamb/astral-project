# Forces, friction and simple machines

The from-zero chapter of Physical Science — everyday forces, not flight. If you've never taken a
physics class, start here; nothing in this chapter assumes you have.

---

## Newton's three laws

| Law | States |
|---|---|
| **First** | An object at rest stays at rest, in motion stays in motion, unless an outside force acts on it (inertia) |
| **Second** | Acceleration = Net force ÷ Mass |
| **Third** | Every action force has an equal and opposite reaction force |

**Mass vs. weight:** mass is the amount of matter in an object. Weight is the force gravity exerts
on that mass — they are not the same thing, even though people use the words interchangeably.

---

## Friction

| Type | Acts on |
|---|---|
| **Static friction** | An object that is **not yet** moving — resists the start of motion |
| **Kinetic friction** | An object that **is already** moving |

Friction always **opposes motion** — that is its entire job, and the fact tested most directly. A
rough surface produces more friction than a smooth one. Friction produces heat as two surfaces rub
together; lubrication reduces it by letting surfaces slide more easily.

## Simple machines

| Machine | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Lever** | A rigid bar pivoting on a fixed point, multiplies force |
| **Inclined plane** | A ramp — reduces the force needed by increasing the distance |
| **Wheel and axle** | Large wheel fixed to a smaller rod, multiplies force turning |
| **Pulley** | Changes the *direction* of an applied force |
| **Wedge** | Thin edge, splits materials apart |
| **Screw** | An inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder |

> ⚠ **A simple machine trades force for distance — it never reduces the total work required.**
> An inclined plane lets you lift something with less force, but you push it over a longer
> distance to make up for it. "Machines make work easier, not less" is the fact behind every
> question in this section.

**Mechanical advantage** is the factor by which a machine multiplies your applied force (the
*effort*) against the resisting force (the *load*).

---

## Equilibrium and net force

| Term | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Balanced forces** | Equal and opposite — no change in motion |
| **Unbalanced forces** | Unequal — motion changes |
| **Net force** | The single combined force from adding all forces on an object |
| **Equilibrium** | The state when all forces on an object are balanced |
| **Tension** | The pulling force through a taut rope or cable |
| **Normal force** | The support force a surface exerts, perpendicular to what rests on it |

> A tug-of-war where neither side moves means the net force is **zero** — the two pulls are equal
> and cancel out. It looks like nothing is happening, but two large forces are actually present
> and fully balanced.
