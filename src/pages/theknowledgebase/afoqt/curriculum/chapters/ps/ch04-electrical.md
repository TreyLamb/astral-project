# Circuits, resistance and magnetism

Four areas: the basics of a circuit, resistance and conductors, the components that make one up,
and magnetism.

---

## Circuit fundamentals

| Term | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Circuit** | A closed loop current can flow through |
| **Current** | The flow of electric charge (amperes) |
| **Voltage** | The electrical pressure pushing current (volts) |
| **Series circuit** | One path — **the same current flows through every component** |
| **Parallel circuit** | Separate branches — current can split |
| **Ohm's law** | Voltage = Current × Resistance |

> ⚠ **In a series circuit, current is the same everywhere — it does not split or increase.** This
> is the single most tested fact about series circuits, and the trap is assuming current changes
> as it "uses up" energy through each component. It doesn't; voltage drops, current doesn't.

---

## Resistance and conductors

| Term | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Conductor** | Lets current flow easily |
| **Insulator** | Strongly resists current |
| **Resistance** | Opposition to current flow (ohms) |
| **Semiconductor** | Conductivity between a conductor and an insulator |
| **Superconductor** | Zero resistance below a critical temperature |

**What changes a wire's resistance:**

| Change | Effect on resistance |
|---|---|
| Better conductor (e.g. copper) | **Decreases** |
| Longer wire | Increases |
| Thinner wire | Increases |
| Higher temperature | Increases |

> ⚠ **Voltage does not affect resistance.** Resistance depends only on the wire's material,
> length, thickness and temperature — not on how much voltage is applied across it. That's the
> distractor this fact is built to catch.

---

## Circuit components

| Component | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Battery** | Stores chemical energy, converts it to electrical energy |
| **Wire** | Carries current between components |
| **Switch** | Opens or closes the path for current |
| **Fuse** | Melts and breaks the path if too much current flows — one-time use |
| **Circuit breaker** | Same job as a fuse, but can be **reset and reused** |
| **Capacitor** | Stores energy temporarily in an electric field |
| **Diode** | Lets current flow in only one direction |
| **Transistor** | Amplifies a signal or switches current using a smaller control current |

> ⚠ **Fuse vs. circuit breaker is a resettability question.** Both protect against excess
> current the same way; the only difference tested is that a fuse is destroyed and replaced, a
> breaker just flips back.

## Magnetism and electromagnetism

| Term | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Magnetic field** | The region around a magnet where its force can be detected |
| **Poles** | Opposite poles attract, like poles repel |
| **Electromagnet** | A magnet whose field comes from current in a coil |
| **Electromagnetic induction** | A *changing* magnetic field generates current in a conductor |
| **Solenoid** | A tightly wound coil producing a strong, uniform field |

| Principle | Direction of conversion |
|---|---|
| **Motor** | Electrical → mechanical (current + field → motion) |
| **Generator** | Mechanical → electrical (motion + field → current) |

> Motor and generator use the exact same physical relationship (current, motion and a magnetic
> field), just run in opposite directions. If you know one, you know the other backwards.

---

## Before you move on

- [ ] In a series circuit, current is identical through every component.
- [ ] A better conductor lowers resistance; length, thinness, and heat all raise it — voltage does not.
- [ ] A fuse is one-time use; a circuit breaker resets.
- [ ] A motor converts electrical energy to motion; a generator does the reverse.
