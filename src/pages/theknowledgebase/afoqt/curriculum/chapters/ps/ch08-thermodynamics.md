# Heat, temperature and the laws of thermodynamics

Three areas: the three ways heat transfers, what the laws of thermodynamics actually say, and
what heating does to a substance's particles.

---

## The three ways heat transfers

| Method | How it moves | Needs a medium? |
|---|---|---|
| **Conduction** | Direct contact between particles | Yes — works best in solids |
| **Convection** | Movement of a fluid (rising warm air/water) | Yes |
| **Radiation** | Electromagnetic waves | **No** — this is how the Sun heats Earth |

**Heat vs. temperature** is a real distinction, not a synonym pair: temperature is the *average*
kinetic energy per particle; heat is the *total* thermal energy actually flowing from a warmer
object to a cooler one.

---

## The laws of thermodynamics

| Law | States |
|---|---|
| **Zeroth** | If A and B are each in equilibrium with C, A and B are in equilibrium with each other |
| **First** | Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted — conservation of energy |
| **Second** | Heat flows spontaneously from warmer to cooler, never the reverse |
| **Third** | Entropy approaches a minimum as temperature approaches absolute zero |

> ⚠ **The first law is conservation of energy, full stop.** It is the single most tested fact in
> this chapter and the most straightforward — resist the temptation to overcomplicate it.

**Entropy** measures disorder, and tends to *increase* over time in an isolated system — that
tendency is what the second law is really describing at the particle level. A **heat engine**
converts thermal energy to mechanical work, but per the second law, it can never convert all of
it — some is always lost.

## What heating does to particles

Heating a substance gives its particles more kinetic energy: they move faster and, in most cases,
spread farther apart. That single fact underlies everything else in this section:

| Term | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Thermal expansion** | Matter grows as it's heated (particles move faster, spread apart) |
| **Thermal contraction** | Matter shrinks as it's cooled |
| **Latent heat** | Energy absorbed/released during a phase change, **with no temperature change** |
| **Expansion joint** | A gap in a bridge/roadway built to allow room for expansion |
| **Bimetallic strip** | Bends when heated — two bonded metals expanding at different rates |

Gases expand far more than solids or liquids for the same temperature rise, since a gas's
particles are already loosely bound. One notable exception worth knowing: **water expands, rather
than contracts, as it freezes** — the opposite of almost every other common substance.

> ⚠ **Latent heat is energy with no temperature change** — that's the counter-intuitive part.
> While ice is melting at 0°C, it is absorbing energy the whole time, but the thermometer doesn't
> move until the phase change is finished.

---

## Before you move on

- [ ] Conduction needs direct contact; convection needs fluid movement; radiation needs neither.
- [ ] The first law is conservation of energy.
- [ ] Heating a substance always gives its particles more kinetic energy.
- [ ] Latent heat changes phase without changing temperature; water is the exception that expands when it freezes.
