# Sound waves and how they travel

Three areas: what a sound wave's properties determine, how sound moves through different media,
and how sound waves bend and shift.

---

## Sound wave properties

| Property | Determines |
|---|---|
| **Frequency** | **Pitch** — how high or low a sound seems |
| **Amplitude** | **Volume** — how loud a sound seems |
| **Timbre** | What lets you tell two sounds of the *same* pitch and volume apart (a violin from a flute) |

> ⚠ **Pitch = frequency. Volume = amplitude.** These two pairings get crossed constantly, and the
> fix is remembering there's no other pairing that makes sense: amplitude is a wave's height
> (loudness), frequency is how often it repeats (pitch).

**Ultrasound** sits above human hearing (~20,000 Hz); **infrasound** sits below it (~20 Hz). The
**decibel scale** measures loudness logarithmically — each 10 dB increase is a *tenfold* rise in
intensity, not a 10% one.

---

## How sound travels

Sound needs a **medium** — unlike light, it cannot cross a vacuum at all.

| Medium | Relative speed |
|---|---|
| **Solid** | Fastest — particles are tightly packed |
| **Liquid** | Middle |
| **Gas** | Slowest — particles are loosely spaced |

As a sound wave moves through a medium, it creates alternating **compressions** (particles pushed
close) and **rarefactions** (particles spread apart) — that pattern of pressure changes is what
sound actually *is*. In air at room temperature, sound travels at roughly 343 m/s, and rising
temperature speeds it up further, since warmer particles transmit vibrations faster.

> ⚠ **Sound is fastest in solids, not slowest.** This runs backwards from a common assumption
> that a "thinner" medium like air would let sound move more freely — it's the opposite, because
> tightly packed particles pass vibrations to each other faster than widely spaced ones.

---

## How sound waves bend and shift

| Term | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Diffraction** | Sound bends around obstacles or through openings — why you can hear someone behind a wall |
| **Reflection** | A wave bounces off a surface at an angle (an echo, if delayed and distinct) |
| **Reverberation** | Many rapid, overlapping reflections in an enclosed space |
| **Doppler effect** | Pitch rises as a source approaches, falls as it recedes |
| **Interference** | Two waves overlap — reinforcing (louder) or canceling (quieter) |
| **Resonance** | An object vibrates at large amplitude because a driving frequency matches its own natural one |

---

## Before you move on

- [ ] Frequency determines pitch; amplitude determines volume.
- [ ] Sound is fastest in solids, slowest in gases, and cannot travel through a vacuum at all.
- [ ] Compressions and rarefactions are the actual pattern a sound wave is made of.
- [ ] Diffraction bends sound around obstacles; the Doppler effect shifts pitch as a source moves.
