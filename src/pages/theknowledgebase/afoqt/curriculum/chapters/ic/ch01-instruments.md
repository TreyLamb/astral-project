# Instrument Comprehension — the method

**25 questions. 5 minutes. 12 seconds each.** Feeds the **Pilot** and **ABM** composites.

Every question is the same: two dials on the left, four aircraft on the right, pick the one the
dials describe. There is no knowledge to learn and no arithmetic to do. There are four
conventions to internalise, and **one of them is deliberately backwards.**

> ⚠ **This subtest also has FOUR answer options, not five.** It is the only one on the test that
> does. A blind guess is worth 25% here rather than 20%, which matters when you are out of time.

---

## Convention 1 — the aircraft on the dial never moves

On a real attitude indicator the little aircraft is fixed and the horizon rolls behind it. Same
here, and the directions are explicit: *"the small aircraft fuselage silhouette remains
stationary, while the positions of the heavy black line and the black pointer vary."*

So do not look at the symbol. Look at **where the horizon line is relative to it**, and **where
the pointer is relative to zero**.

---

## Convention 2 — ⚠ the pointer is INVERTED

This is the trap the whole subtest is built on. Straight from the directions:

> *"If the airplane is banked to the pilot's **right**, the pointer is seen to the **left** of
> zero. If the airplane is banked to the pilot's **left**, the pointer is seen to the **right**
> of zero."*

| Pointer sits… | The aircraft is banked… |
|---|---|
| **Left** of zero | **RIGHT** |
| **Right** of zero | **LEFT** |
| On zero | Not at all |

Say it out loud a few times: **pointer left means banked right.** Every question that trips
people trips them here, and one of your four options is always the correctly-read aircraft banked
the wrong way.

The **horizon line is always at right angles to the pointer**, so it tilts by the same amount.
That is a useful cross-check: if the line is tilted and the pointer is on zero, you have misread
something.

---

## Convention 3 — climb and dive by what sits in the middle

Both cases put three things in a line — the horizon line, the silhouette and the pointer — and
which one is **in the middle** tells you the answer:

| What is in the middle | The aircraft is |
|---|---|
| The **silhouette** | **Climbing** |
| The **horizon line** | **Diving** |

> *"If the airplane is climbing, the fuselage silhouette is seen between the horizon line and the
> pointer… If the airplane is diving, the horizon line is seen between the fuselage silhouette
> and the pointer."*

And the **greater the separation, the steeper** the climb or dive. Level flight puts the horizon
line directly across the silhouette.

---

## Convention 4 — ⭐ you are always looking north

This is the one that decides the answer, and it is stated in capitals in the official directions:

> **"YOU ARE ALWAYS LOOKING NORTH AT THE SAME ALTITUDE AS EACH OF THE FOUR AIRPLANES. EAST IS
> ALWAYS TO YOUR RIGHT AS YOU LOOK AT THE PAGE."**

Work out what that means once and you never have to work it out again:

| Compass says | You see |
|---|---|
| **North** | The aircraft flying **away** from you — a **rear view** |
| **South** | The aircraft flying **toward** you — a **front view** |
| **East** | The aircraft flying to your **right** |
| **West** | The aircraft flying to your **left** |

Anything in between is an oblique view — a northeast heading is going away and to the right.

---

## The four options are a fixed formula

The wrong answers are not random. The official sample names them, and they are the same three
mistakes every time:

| Option | The mistake it catches |
|---|---|
| The correct aircraft | — |
| A **rear view** | You read the compass as north |
| A **front view** | You read the compass as south |
| The **wrong bank** | You forgot the pointer is inverted |

That is genuinely useful under time pressure. If two of your four candidates are a plain front
view and a plain rear view, **the compass is the thing to read first** — it eliminates half the
options in about two seconds. Then the bank pointer picks between the remaining two.

---

## The order to read them in

1. **Compass first.** It halves the field faster than anything else.
2. **Bank pointer second** — and remember it is inverted. This usually settles it.
3. **Horizon line last**, for climb or dive, if two options are still standing.

Twelve seconds is enough for that sequence and not enough to stare. If you are still undecided at
eight seconds, take the option that matches the compass and move on — with four options and no
guessing penalty, a partial read still beats a blank.

---

## Before you move on

- [ ] **Pointer left = banked right.** Automatic, no thinking.
- [ ] Silhouette in the middle = climbing; horizon line in the middle = diving.
- [ ] Heading north = rear view, heading south = front view, east = to your right.
- [ ] You read the compass first, then the pointer, then the horizon.
- [ ] You know there are only four options here, so a guess is worth 25%.
