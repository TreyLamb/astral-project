## Baseline — updated after the Aug 1–2, 2026 time trials

The plan below was originally built on an assumed 20:00 (10:00/mile) 2-mile baseline. Actual time trial data (1600m, 1200m, aborted-800m-treated-as-400m — full numbers logged in `training-context.md`) put the real working baseline closer to **~18:00 for 2 miles**, roughly two minutes faster than assumed, done in 91°F heat at ~4,575 ft elevation (so true cool-weather fitness is a touch better still).

**What the numbers say about where the limiter is:** it's not top-end speed — a fresh first 400m came in at 1:56, already close to the 1:45 goal race pace. The limiter is aerobic endurance / ability to sustain hard effort: heart rate ran 185 avg / 197 peak on the mile effort, and the 1200m wasn't faster per-lap than the 1600m (a fade, not a lack of speed). This confirms the plan's core structure — aerobic base and durability first, speed work isn't the bottleneck — it just means the whole curve starts about two minutes ahead of where it was assumed to be.

**Current settled training paces (use these now; recalculate after each checkpoint test):**
- Easy / long run: **~10:45–11:15/mile**, by feel, HR controlled — this is the biggest lever, don't let it creep faster.
- Threshold (comfortably hard): **~9:00–9:15/mile**.
- Speed reps (400m): **~2:00 now**, full recovery, building toward 1:45.
- Goal race pace: **1:45/400m = 14:00 for 2 miles**.
- Cycle 1 specifically (base phase, coming off time-trial soreness): keep both speed days light — strides + a few relaxed ~2:00 400s, full recovery, nothing near max. No formal threshold yet.

**HR flag (carried over, not urgent):** 180–185 after the first mile at "maintainable" effort is high for that effort level. Could just be a currently-low aerobic base (fixable — that's what Phase 1 is for), could be an easy pace that's been run too hot, could be a personal HR quirk. Worth knowing resting HR and, if it's been a while, a basic checkup — not because anything sounds wrong, just cheap to check. Not medical advice.

## The core problem with "just add speed work"

The gap to close isn't primarily speed — a 9:00/mile runner and a 7:00/mile runner aren't separated mainly by top-end speed, they're separated by aerobic capacity, running economy, and the ability to sustain hard effort. That's why this plan spends its first ~40% of duration on aerobic base and durability before threshold work, and doesn't touch real race-pace specificity until the final phase.

## The 3 phases (unchanged in concept)

### Phase 1 — Rebuild Durability (Cycles 1–5, ~Weeks 1–8)
Run consistently without digging a fatigue hole. Build long run from 2mi toward 4.5mi. No formal threshold or hill work yet — strides only.

### Phase 2 — Aerobic Strength & Threshold (Cycles 6–10, ~Weeks 9–16)
Make "comfortably hard" pace feel normal. Formal threshold sessions begin. Hills reintroduced *very* gradually here at the earliest, given ankle history — never sustained hill running, only short controlled hill sprints once base is solid.

### Phase 3 — 2-Mile Specificity & Taper (Cycles 11–14, ~Weeks 17–22)
Train the actual demands of the test. Threshold replaced by race-pace and faster-than-race-pace repeats (400s/800s). Volume comes down as intensity peaks. Final cycle tapers into test day.

### How to set actual pace each cycle
After every checkpoint test, recalculate threshold/interval paces off that new real number (roughly: threshold effort ≈ current 2-mile pace + 45–60 sec/mile; race-pace reps ≈ goal pace for that stage). Don't run intervals off an old baseline once you've moved past it.

## Hills

Genuinely useful, not mandatory for a flat 2-mile test. Given ankle history, no hill work until Phase 2 at the earliest (Cycle 6+), and even then only short controlled sprints on a gentle grade with full recovery — never sustained hill running. If the ankle flares at all, drop hills and substitute flat sprints.

---

## Guardrails

- **Ankle:** any sharp pain (not normal muscle fatigue) is a stop sign for that session.
- **Deload/checkpoint week shows no improvement or feels worse than last checkpoint:** extend Phase 1 or 2 rather than force the calendar.
- **Two hard days should never sit back-to-back** — `cycle.md` already spaces them; protect that spacing over anything else if life reshuffles the week.
- Not medical or physical-therapy guidance — anything that flares up meaningfully is worth a professional look.
