## Policies & Procedures

### Communication Etiquette (p.12–13) ✅

Communication is a vital tool relied on throughout an AF career; ROTC is where proper etiquette is
learned and practiced.

1. **Email** is the preferred method of communication with ROTC personnel.
   - Check school email multiple times per day; reply as soon as practical. Even a simple
     acknowledgment-of-receipt reply beats letting more than **24 hours** pass without replying.
   - Enable email notifications on your phone so you don't miss opportunities/important notices.
   - **Proper email format, GMC cadets:**
     ```
     Good <morning, afternoon, evening> ___,

     Insert Message.


     Very Respectfully,

     <FIRST M. LAST>, C/4C, USAF
     AFROTC Detachment 855
     <(123) 456-7890>
     <School Email>
     ```
   - **Proper email format, POC cadets** (signature block, from the example given):
     ```
     Very Respectfully,

     MATTHEW T. CLARKSON, C/Maj, USAF
     Standards & Evaluation Officer
     AFROTC Detachment 855
     (435) 899-8459
     matt77@byu.edu
     ```
     General POC signature pattern: `<FIRST M. LAST>, C/<Rank>, USAF` / `<Cadet Position, as
     printed on org chart>` / `AFROTC Detachment 855` / `<phone>` / `<school email>`.
2. **Teams.** The "Det 855 Cadets" Teams channel is used by cadet leadership and cadre to post
   announcements about upcoming events. Enable notifications; check both the channel and your
   Teams chats regularly.
3. **Recall.** Periodically the detachment conducts a "recall" to check accountability — a text
   from your cadet flight commander that you reply to promptly with your location and status
   (okay or not). Purpose: quickly establish accountability of everyone in the detachment during
   an emergency (natural disaster, active shooter, etc.).

## Warrior Knowledge

### Saluting Procedures (p.38) ✅

Brief history: the hand salute evolved from medieval knights lifting their visor to show no
weapon/recognition as a friend. Today it says, in effect, "I greet you" / "I return your
greeting" — always rendered willingly, with pride and respect, as a sign of recognition between
comrades in arms.

**Whom to salute:** Tradition holds the junior-ranking person salutes first. The one exception:
when a unit commander gives an official report to an adjutant who might be junior — that senior
member's the one who initiates in that specific case. The person saluted always returns it,
unless physically unable (incapacity, or the right hand isn't free — e.g. carrying packages).

As a cadet, in uniform, you must salute:
- Any commissioned or warrant officer of the Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, or Coast Guard
- Commissioned officers of friendly foreign countries
- All senior-ranking cadet officers in Air Force ROTC

"If you can't identify the rank of an individual or remember if they rate a salute, give one
anyway. It is better to be safe than sorry. Just remember the following advice: 'When in doubt,
salute.'"

### Formal Reporting-In Procedures (p.39) ✅

**1) Single cadet reporting in:**
- a) Center on the door or cubicle entrance and knock once — loud enough to be heard in an
  average-size room. Do NOT knock on the door/wall if a conversation is already taking place in
  the office.
- b) When given the order, enter, close the door (if you opened it), march the most direct route
  to the staff member, perform the appropriate facing movement (a crisp military turn if on
  carpet), center yourself two paces away. Salute if reporting to an officer or POC, and state:
  **"Sir/Ma'am, Cadet (last name) reports as ordered."**
- c) When reporting to an officer or POC, hold the salute until they drop theirs, and remain at
  the position of attention until told otherwise.
- d) At the end of the conversation: come to attention (if at a different position) and ask,
  **"Will that be all, Sir/Ma'am?"** If so: step back one pace, salute (if reporting to an
  officer/POC), render the proper greeting of the day, execute the facing movement to leave, and
  take the most direct route out (a crisp turn if on carpet).

**2) Multiple cadets reporting in together:**
- a) A selected cadet centers on the door/cubicle entrance and knocks once, same rules as above.
- b) When given the order, ALL cadets enter, close the door, and center two paces away from the
  individual — with the SELECTED cadet standing on the right of the group. Salute if reporting to
  an officer/POC. The selected cadet states on behalf of the group: **"Sir/Ma'am, Cadets (last
  names of the whole party) report as ordered."**
