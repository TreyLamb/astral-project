### Structure of AFROTC (p.8) 📘

- **Program:** AFROTC is run by HQ AFROTC under the Holm Center, which also oversees Officer
  Training School, AFJROTC and Civil Air Patrol-USAF. The Holm Center produces about 85% of
  the officers entering the Department of the Air Force total force.
- **Two courses:** the **General Military Course (GMC)** is AS100 and AS200, the first two years;
  the **Professional Officer Course (POC)** is AS300 and AS400. Field Training sits between them.
- **What is graded:** Aerospace Studies class (AS), Leadership Laboratory (LLAB) and Physical
  Training (PT). AFROTCI 36-2011 Vol 3 para 9.9.1 sets a minimum 80% attendance in each; Det 855's
  own memo says the minimum is the floor and the expectation is 100%.
- **A detachment** is the equivalent of a squadron. Its cadre are the active-duty staff (Det
  Commander, Operations Flight Commander, and so on).
- **Det 855 cadet wing** (OPLAN F26): the Cadet Wing sits over a Training Group (X-Ray, Yankee and
  Zulu Squadrons, with Flights A to F), an Operations Group (ODC Task Force, Physical Training
  Squadron, LLAB Squadron) and a Mission Generation Group (Public Affairs, Force Support and
  Cyber Squadrons). Charlie Flight is in Yankee Squadron.

## Policies & Procedures

### Customs & Courtesies (p.10–11) 📘

Saluting and reporting-in are in their own sections below.

- **Titles of address.** Officers: rank + last name, or "Sir/Ma'am." NCOs: rank (+ last name);
  never "Sir/Ma'am." Cadets: "Cadet" + last name. Cadets address cadre by rank + last name or
  "Sir/Ma'am."
- **Position of honor.** The senior takes the right; a junior walks, sits or rides on the
  senior's left, slightly behind. Customary rule for vehicles: the senior boards last and exits
  first. (Customary, not a single codified regulation.)
- **Calling a room to attention.** The first person to notice a senior officer entering calls
  "Room, ATTENTION." The officer then says "Carry on" or "As you were." Do not call it again for
  an officer who is not senior to someone already in the room.
- **Standing.** Stand when addressed by an officer, NCO or senior cadet while seated, and when
  cadre enter or leave the room.
- **Reveille and Retreat.** Reveille marks the start of the duty day; Retreat marks its end and
  the flag lowering. In uniform and outdoors, stop and stand at parade rest for the bugle call,
  then come to attention and salute for "To the Colors" or the anthem, holding until the last
  note. In civilian clothes, stand at attention with the right hand over the heart. A vehicle
  pulls over and stops.
- **National Anthem.** In uniform and outdoors: face the flag (or the music), attention, salute
  from the first note to the last. Indoors, in uniform, bareheaded: attention only, no salute;
  with headgear in formation, or under arms: salute.
- **Pledge of Allegiance.** Under 4 U.S.C. § 4, a person in uniform stands at attention, faces the
  flag, stays silent and salutes. Secondary sources vary on this one; this is the statute's answer.
- **Passing colors.** Salute when the uncased national color is six paces away and hold until it
  is six paces past.
- **Receiving an award.** Shake (right hand), take (left hand), salute.
- **Headgear.** Off indoors, except under arms or on an armed detail.

### Communication Etiquette (p.12–13) ✅

Communication is a vital tool relied on throughout an AF career; ROTC is where proper etiquette is
learned and practiced.

1. **Email** is the preferred method of communication with ROTC personnel.
   - Check school email multiple times per day; reply as soon as practical. Even a simple
     acknowledgment-of-receipt reply beats letting more than **24 hours** pass without replying.
   - Enable email notifications on your phone so you don't miss opportunities/important notices.
   - **Proper email format, GMC cadets:**
     ```
     Good <morning, afternoon, evening> ___,

     Insert Message.


     Very Respectfully,

     <FIRST M. LAST>, C/4C, USAF
     AFROTC Detachment 855
     <(123) 456-7890>
     <School Email>
     ```
   - **Proper email format, POC cadets** (signature block, from the example given):
     ```
     Very Respectfully,

     MATTHEW T. CLARKSON, C/Maj, USAF
     Standards & Evaluation Officer
     AFROTC Detachment 855
     (435) 899-8459
     matt77@byu.edu
     ```
     General POC signature pattern: `<FIRST M. LAST>, C/<Rank>, USAF` / `<Cadet Position, as
     printed on org chart>` / `AFROTC Detachment 855` / `<phone>` / `<school email>`.
2. **Teams.** The "Det 855 Cadets" Teams channel is used by cadet leadership and cadre to post
   announcements about upcoming events. Enable notifications; check both the channel and your
   Teams chats regularly.
3. **Recall.** Periodically the detachment conducts a "recall" to check accountability — a text
   from your cadet flight commander that you reply to promptly with your location and status
   (okay or not). Purpose: quickly establish accountability of everyone in the detachment during
   an emergency (natural disaster, active shooter, etc.).

## Warrior Knowledge

### Air Force Core Values (p.15) 📘

1. **Integrity First.** Doing the right thing, all the time, whether or not anyone is watching.
   It is the foundation trust and respect are built on.
2. **Service Before Self.** Holding yourself to a higher standard as a daily commitment, without
   giving up family, loved ones or your own beliefs.
3. **Excellence In All We Do.** Not literal perfection. A passion for continuously improving your
   craft and your performance.

### Space Force Core Values (p.17) 📘

The Guardian Ideal, unveiled by Gen John W. Raymond in Sep 2021.

1. **Character…Above All.** High ethical standards; act with integrity; stay accountable and honor
   your obligations.
2. **Connection…Toward Unity.** Stronger together than alone; treat everyone with empathy and respect.
3. **Commitment…To Mastery.** Mastering yourself, your profession and your domain.
4. **Courage…To Be Bold.** Standing up for what is right; a bias toward action; accepting risk when
   necessary.

Members of the Space Force are called **Guardians**. Space Force mission: "Secure our Nation's
interests in, from, and to space." Its three core functions are Space Superiority ("Control the
Domain"), Global Mission Operations ("Exploit the Domain") and Assured Space Access ("Access the
Domain").

### Code of Conduct (p.21) 📘

Established by President Eisenhower in **Executive Order 10631 (17 Aug 1955)**, amended by EO 12017
(1977) and EO 12633 (1988).

- **I.** I am an American, fighting in the forces which guard my country and our way of life. I am
  prepared to give my life in their defense.
- **II.** I will never surrender of my own free will. If in command, I will never surrender the
  members of my command while they still have the means to resist.
- **III.** If I am captured I will continue to resist by all means available. I will make every
  effort to escape and aid others to escape. I will accept neither parole nor special favors from
  the enemy.
- **IV.** If I become a prisoner of war, I will keep faith with my fellow prisoners. I will give no
  information or take part in any action which might be harmful to my comrades. If I am senior, I
  will take command. If not, I will obey the lawful orders of those appointed over me and will back
  them up in every way.
- **V.** When questioned, should I become a prisoner of war, I am required to give name, rank,
  service number and date of birth. I will evade answering further questions to the utmost of my
  ability. I will make no oral or written statements disloyal to my country and its allies or
  harmful to their cause.
- **VI.** I will never forget that I am an American, fighting for freedom, responsible for my
  actions, and dedicated to the principles which made my country free. I will trust in my God and
  in the United States of America.

### Cadet Ranks (p.25) 📘

There are **eight** AFROTC cadet grades and no cadet enlisted ranks (AFROTCI 36-2011 Vol 3 para
10.7.5). No cadet may hold a grade above Cadet Colonel. Stripes below read from the outer edge in;
"wide" and "narrow" are the stripe widths.

- **C/4C, Cadet Fourth Class** (AS100, GMC): blue and silver, worn point toward the neck.
- **C/3C, Cadet Third Class** (AS200, GMC): every cadet wears C/3C at Field Training, whatever
  their detachment rank.
- **C/2Lt, Cadet Second Lieutenant** (POC): one wide stripe.
- **C/1Lt, Cadet First Lieutenant** (POC): one wide plus one narrow.
- **C/Capt, Cadet Captain** (POC): two equal wide stripes.
- **C/Maj, Cadet Major** (POC): wide, narrow, wide.
- **C/Lt Col, Cadet Lieutenant Colonel** (POC): three equal wide stripes.
- **C/Col, Cadet Colonel** (POC): four wide stripes. Normally the Cadet Wing Commander.

Confusable pairs: C/1Lt vs C/Capt (two marks) and C/Maj vs C/Lt Col (three marks) differ only in
stripe width. The abbreviations on the official chart are C/2Lt and C/1Lt; the Fall 2026 OPLAN
writes them "C/2d Lt" and "C/1st Lt."

### Enlisted Rank Structure, USAF (p.26) 📘

Total stripe count is the pay grade minus 1 (E-6 has 5).

- **E-1 Airman Basic (AB):** no insignia
- **E-2 Airman (Amn):** 1 stripe
- **E-3 Airman First Class (A1C):** 2 stripes
- **E-4 Senior Airman (SrA):** 3 stripes. Not an NCO.
- **E-5 Staff Sergeant (SSgt):** first NCO grade
- **E-6 Technical Sergeant (TSgt)**
- **E-7 Master Sergeant (MSgt):** first Senior NCO (SNCO) grade
- **E-8 Senior Master Sergeant (SMSgt)**
- **E-9 Chief Master Sergeant (CMSgt)**
- **Chief Master Sergeant of the Air Force (CMSAF):** the one unique enlisted position above E-9,
  and the only Air Force enlisted insignia with a wreath.
- **First Sergeant** is a duty position (E-7 to E-9) shown by a diamond. A **Command Chief** wears
  the CMSgt chevron with one extra star.

Reminder: Air Force E-8 and E-9 have no wreath. That is an Army, Marine Corps and Navy convention.

### Enlisted Rank Structure, USSF (p.27) 📘

- **E-1 to E-4: Specialist 1, 2, 3, 4** (not Airman titles)
- **E-5: Sergeant.** This is the one NCO-level grade where titles split: USAF has Staff Sergeant.
- **E-6 to E-9:** Technical, Master, Senior Master and Chief Master Sergeant, the same as the USAF.
- The senior enlisted leader is the Chief Master Sergeant of the Space Force (CMSSF).

### Officer Rank Structure (p.28) 📘

Same titles and metal devices in the Air Force and Space Force. Within each pair, gold is junior
and silver is senior. There is no gold eagle and no gold star.

- **O-1 Second Lieutenant (2d Lt):** one gold bar
- **O-2 First Lieutenant (1st Lt):** one silver bar
- **O-3 Captain (Capt):** two silver bars
- **O-4 Major (Maj):** gold oak leaf
- **O-5 Lieutenant Colonel (Lt Col):** silver oak leaf
- **O-6 Colonel (Col):** silver eagle
- **O-7 Brigadier General (Brig Gen):** 1 star
- **O-8 Major General (Maj Gen):** 2 stars
- **O-9 Lieutenant General (Lt Gen):** 3 stars
- **O-10 General (Gen):** 4 stars

Verbal address: a Lieutenant Colonel is "Colonel"; both lieutenants are "Lieutenant"; every general
is "General."

Cross-service traps: a Navy **Captain (O-6)** is three grades above an Air Force Captain (O-3); a
Navy Lieutenant (LT) is an O-3; Navy Ensign is the O-1; Navy and Coast Guard flag officers are
Admirals, and Rear Admiral Lower Half (1 star, O-7) and Upper Half (2 stars, O-8) share a title.

### Oath of Office & Oath of Enlistment (p.29) 📘

**Oath of Enlistment, 10 U.S.C. § 502:** "I, ______, do solemnly swear (or affirm) that I will
support and defend the Constitution of the United States against all enemies, foreign and
domestic; that I will bear true faith and allegiance to the same; and that I will obey the orders
of the President of the United States and the orders of the officers appointed over me, according
to regulations and the Uniform Code of Military Justice. So help me God."

**Oath of Office, 5 U.S.C. § 3331** (taken by commissioned officers): "I, AB, do solemnly swear
(or affirm) that I will support and defend the Constitution of the United States against all
enemies, foreign and domestic; that I will bear true faith and allegiance to the same; that I
take this obligation freely, without any mental reservation or purpose of evasion; and that I
will well and faithfully discharge the duties of the office on which I am about to enter. So help
me God."

The difference to remember: the enlistment oath adds obeying the President and the officers
appointed over you (and the UCMJ); the officer oath adds taking the obligation "freely, without
any mental reservation or purpose of evasion" and faithfully discharging the duties of the office.
"So help me God" may be omitted by anyone who chooses.

### Air Force Major Commands (p.32) 📘

Nine MAJCOMs (checked 17 Oct 2026). Seven are functional and two are geographic. The Air National Guard is not a MAJCOM. Space
organizations belong to the Space Force and are on the next page.

- **Air Combat Command (ACC):** Langley AFB, VA
- **Air Education and Training Command (AETC):** Randolph AFB (JBSA), TX. AFROTC's parent command.
- **Air Force Global Strike Command (AFGSC):** Barksdale AFB, LA. The newest MAJCOM (Aug 2009).
- **Air Force Materiel Command (AFMC):** Wright-Patterson AFB, OH
- **Air Force Reserve Command (AFRC):** Robins AFB, GA
- **Air Force Special Operations Command (AFSOC):** Hurlburt Field, FL
- **Air Mobility Command (AMC):** Scott AFB, IL
- **Pacific Air Forces (PACAF):** Joint Base Pearl Harbor-Hickam, HI (geographic)
- **U.S. Air Forces in Europe – Air Forces Africa (USAFE-AFAFRICA):** Ramstein Air Base, Germany (geographic)

Institutional MAJCOMs organize, train and equip Airmen (for example AETC, ACC). Service
component MAJCOMs prepare Airmen to fight in a combatant command's area of responsibility.

### Space Force Field Commands (p.33) 📘

Three field commands. ⚠️ **Space Operations Command (SpOC) was renamed Combat Forces Command
(CFC) on 3 Nov 2025.** Most study guides still say SpOC.

- **Combat Forces Command (CFC)**, formerly SpOC: Peterson SFB, CO. Develops tactics, techniques,
  procedures and force-generation models; generates and presents space forces for combatant
  commanders.
- **Space Systems Command (SSC):** Los Angeles AFB, CA. Acquisition: develops, buys, fields and
  sustains space capabilities.
- **Space Training and Readiness Command (STARCOM):** Patrick SFB, FL. Education, training,
  doctrine and test to build Guardian readiness.

Echelons, smallest to largest: Squadron → Delta → Field Command → Headquarters. The Chief of Space
Operations (a four-star) reports to the Secretary of the Air Force.

### Military Time (p.35) 📘

- The 24-hour clock is written as four digits with no colon: 0000 to 2359.
- Before noon it looks like the 12-hour clock without the colon: 0600 is 6:00 AM. After noon, add 12
  to the hour: 1300 is 1:00 PM, 1830 is 6:30 PM, 2200 is 10:00 PM.
- Midnight is 0000 (start of the day); 2359 is the last minute of the day.
- Said aloud: 0600 is "zero six hundred"; 1830 is "eighteen thirty"; 0005 is "zero zero zero five."
- A trailing letter names the time zone: **Z (Zulu)** is UTC. The Utah time zone is Mountain.
- Example: PT at 0600 and LLAB at 1100 are the times the Det 855 attendance memo uses.

### Saluting Procedures (p.38) ✅

Brief history: the hand salute evolved from medieval knights lifting their visor to show no
weapon/recognition as a friend. Today it says, in effect, "I greet you" / "I return your
greeting" — always rendered willingly, with pride and respect, as a sign of recognition between
comrades in arms.

**Whom to salute:** Tradition holds the junior-ranking person salutes first. The one exception:
when a unit commander gives an official report to an adjutant who might be junior — that senior
member's the one who initiates in that specific case. The person saluted always returns it,
unless physically unable (incapacity, or the right hand isn't free — e.g. carrying packages).

As a cadet, in uniform, you must salute:
- Any commissioned or warrant officer of the Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, or Coast Guard
- Commissioned officers of friendly foreign countries
- All senior-ranking cadet officers in Air Force ROTC

"If you can't identify the rank of an individual or remember if they rate a salute, give one
anyway. It is better to be safe than sorry. Just remember the following advice: 'When in doubt,
salute.'"

### Formal Reporting-In Procedures (p.39) ✅

**1) Single cadet reporting in:**
- a) Center on the door or cubicle entrance and knock once — loud enough to be heard in an
  average-size room. Do NOT knock on the door/wall if a conversation is already taking place in
  the office.
- b) When given the order, enter, close the door (if you opened it), march the most direct route
  to the staff member, perform the appropriate facing movement (a crisp military turn if on
  carpet), center yourself two paces away. Salute if reporting to an officer or POC, and state:
  **"Sir/Ma'am, Cadet (last name) reports as ordered."**
- c) When reporting to an officer or POC, hold the salute until they drop theirs, and remain at
  the position of attention until told otherwise.
- d) At the end of the conversation: come to attention (if at a different position) and ask,
  **"Will that be all, Sir/Ma'am?"** If so: step back one pace, salute (if reporting to an
  officer/POC), render the proper greeting of the day, execute the facing movement to leave, and
  take the most direct route out (a crisp turn if on carpet).

**2) Multiple cadets reporting in together:**
- a) A selected cadet centers on the door/cubicle entrance and knocks once, same rules as above.
- b) When given the order, ALL cadets enter, close the door, and center two paces away from the
  individual — with the SELECTED cadet standing on the right of the group. Salute if reporting to
  an officer/POC. The selected cadet states on behalf of the group: **"Sir/Ma'am, Cadets (last
  names of the whole party) report as ordered."**
