## A) DRILL FUNDAMENTALS (SOB 7.1)

### Anatomy of a drill command [DAFPAM 34-1203, para 2.1]

Most drill commands have **two parts**:

1. **Preparatory Command** — explains *what* the movement will be. Printed with first letter capitalized/boldface in the manual (e.g., **Squadron**, **Forward**, **Right**). When calling a unit to attention or halting its march, the preparatory command includes the unit's designation (e.g., in "Flight, HALT," the word *Flight* is the preparatory command and simultaneously designates the unit).
2. **Command of Execution** — explains *when* the movement is carried out. Printed in all caps/boldface (e.g., **ATTENTION**, **MARCH**, **FACE**). In "Forward, MARCH," the command of execution is MARCH.

**Cadence/interval between the two parts** [para 2.4.1]: "The interval producing the best effect in a movement is the one allowing **one step** between the preparatory command and the command of execution." For a squadron or larger unit, lengthen the interval enough to allow the marching elements to take **three steps** between the commander's preparatory command and command of execution [para 2.4.3, 2.2.4].

**Inflection** [para 2.3.4]:
- Preparatory command: given with a **rising inflection** near/at the end (usually the last syllable), starting near the natural speaking pitch (a common fault is starting too high, leaving no room to rise further for the command of execution).
- Command of execution: **no inflection** — delivered flat, but at a **higher pitch** than the preparatory command, with **snap** — given at "the precise instant the heel of the proper foot strikes the ground while marching" [para 2.3.5].

**Which foot** [para 2.2.7, 3.9.2]: "The preparatory command and the command of execution are given as the heel of the foot corresponding to the direction of the movement strikes the ground." For units no larger than a flight, the preparatory command is given as the relevant heel strikes the ground, and the command of execution is given the *next* time that same heel strikes the ground.

**Note on "back step":** DAFPAM 34-1203 does **not** use the term "back step" as a named movement/command of execution. The equivalent action is the command "**\_\_\_\_ Steps Backward, MARCH**," used only for short repositioning (four steps/paces or less) while forming a squadron — 12-inch steps, left foot first, normal arm swing [para 5.3.2]. If a study guide or test uses "back step" as a term, the 12-inch figure should still be correct, but be aware the current manual's actual command language is "Steps Backward," not "Back Step, MARCH."

### Types of commands [DAFPAM 34-1203, para 2.1]

1. **Two-part commands** (the standard form): preparatory command + command of execution, e.g., *Forward, MARCH*; *Right, FACE*.
2. **Combined commands**: the preparatory command and command of execution are fused into a single utterance. The manual's explicit examples are **FALL IN**, **AT EASE**, and **REST**. These are given "at a uniformly high pitch and a louder volume than that of a normal command of execution" [para 2.1.1.3].
3. **Supplementary commands**: given when one unit must execute a *different* movement than others, or the *same* movement at a *different* time. Examples given in the manual: **CONTINUE THE MARCH** and **STAND FAST** [para 2.1.2]. Given over the right shoulder by the subordinate commander, between the higher commander's preparatory command and command of execution [para 2.2.3].
4. **Informational commands**: have **no** preparatory command or command of execution and are not supplementary. The manual's listed examples: **PREPARE FOR INSPECTION**, **COLUMN OF FILES**, and **DISMISS THE SQUADRON** [para 2.1.3]. (Note: the source text literally says "Two examples are..." but then lists three — this appears to be an editing artifact in the official DAFPAM itself, not a transcription error here.)

### "As you were," revoking, and correcting a command [para 2.2.5]

- **AS YOU WERE** is used to **revoke a preparatory command** (i.e., before the command of execution is given / before the movement begins).
- If the command of execution has **already** been given and the movement has **already begun**, the leader does **not** say "as you were" — instead, they give **other appropriate commands** to bring the element to the desired position.
- **If a command is improperly given**, individuals execute the movement **to the best of their ability** (i.e., troops are not faulted for correctly executing a badly-given command).

### Position of Attention — VERIFIED, precise text [para 3.2]

> "Bring the heels together smartly and in line with one another. Place the heels as near each other as the conformation of the body permits, and ensure the feet are turned out equally, forming a **45-degree angle**. Keep the legs straight without stiffening or locking the knees. The body is erect with hips level, chest lifted, back arched, and shoulders square and even. Arms hang straight down alongside the body without stiffness, and the wrists are straight with the forearms. Place thumbs, which are resting along the first joint of the forefinger, along the seams of the trousers or sides of the skirt. Hands are cupped (but not clenched as a fist) with palms facing the leg. The head is kept erect and held straight to the front with the chin drawn in slightly so the axis of the head and neck is vertical; eyes are to the front, with the line of sight parallel to the ground. The weight of the body rests equally on the heels and balls of both feet, and silence and immobility are required."

Key exact facts for testing:
- Feet form a **45-degree angle**.
- Knees straight, **not locked/stiffened**.
- Thumbs along trouser seam, resting along the first joint of the forefinger.
- Weight distributed **equally on heels and balls of both feet** (not "all on the balls" or "all on the heels").
- Chin drawn in slightly, eyes front, line of sight **parallel to the ground**.

### Rest positions — VERIFIED, this is the highest-yield item [para 3.3]

All rests are executed **only from the position of attention**.

| Position | Command | Right foot must stay in place? | Talking allowed? | Key mechanics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Parade Rest** | *Parade,* **REST** | N/A (left foot moves) | **No** — silent | Left foot moves 12 inches left (heel to heel, measured inside-heel to inside-heel); legs straight not stiff; hands form an "X" in the small of the back, right hand in left palm, right thumb over left; head/eyes stay front; silent and immobile |
| **At Ease** | **AT EASE** | **Yes — right foot stays in place** | **No** — "silence will be maintained" | Relax in a standing position; position in formation does not change |
| **Rest** | **REST** | **Yes — right foot stays in place** | **Yes** — "moderate speech is permitted" | Same physical requirements as At Ease, but talking allowed |
| **Fall Out** | **FALL OUT** | No — may break ranks entirely | **Yes** — moderate speech permitted | May relax standing or leave ranks; must stay in the immediate area; no set method of dispersal required |

**The critical distinction the test will probe:** *At Ease* and *Rest* are physically identical (right foot locked in place, position in formation unchanged) — the **only** difference is that *At Ease* requires silence and *Rest* allows moderate speech. *Fall Out* is the only one of the four where a person may actually leave their position/break ranks.

To return to attention from any rest **except Fall Out**: the command is (unit), **ATTENTION** — e.g., "Flight, ATTENTION." On the preparatory command ("Flight"), assume parade rest first (if not already there); on "ATTENTION," assume attention [para 3.3.2].

## B) INDIVIDUAL DRILL — STATIONARY AND MARCHING MOVEMENTS (SOB 7.2 / 7.3)

**Important scope note verified directly from the AFROTC curriculum** [AFROTCI 36-2011, Vol 1, para 7.2.1 and 7.2.5]: SOB 7.2 ("Demonstrate individual drill instruction") **officially covers only stationary positions**:
- Attention and Parade Rest
- Right/Left Face
- About Face
- Present/Order Arms

The curriculum states explicitly: *"This lesson is intended to be instructed while individuals or flights are stationary rather than while marching."* **Marching movements (Forward March, Halt, flanking, column movements, To the Rear, Half Step, Mark Time, Change Step) are covered under SOB 7.3, "Demonstrate basic drill of the flight,"** not 7.2, per the current curriculum's own organization. Since the graded test description given to you explicitly asks about marching movements under "7.2," they are included in full below — just be aware that if the cadet's actual grading sheet separates these strictly by SOB number, marching items may formally sit under 7.3 rather than 7.2.

### Stationary facing movements [para 3.4]

All facing movements are executed **from a halt**, at attention, **in quick time, in two counts**.

- **Right (Left) Face** — Command: *Right (Left),* **FACE**. Count 1: raise the right (left) toe and left (right) heel slightly, pivot **90 degrees** on the ball of the left (right) foot and heel of the right (left) foot. Count 2: bring the trailing foot smartly alongside, heels together, resuming the 45-degree attention stance.
- **About Face** — Command: *About,* **FACE**. Count 1: lift the right foot, place the ball of the right foot about a half shoe-length behind and slightly left of the left heel (weight distributed on ball of right foot / heel of left foot) — the foot's position doesn't change yet. Count 2: pivot **180 degrees** to the right on the ball of the right foot and heel of the left foot, twisting from the hips, arm swing suspended; finish at attention.
- **Half Right (Left) Face** — same mechanics as Right/Left Face but a **45-degree** pivot.

### Hand Salute / Present Arms / Order Arms [para 3.5, 3.7]

- **Hand Salute** — Command: *HAND,* **SALUTE**. Two counts. Count 1 (on SALUTE): raise the right hand smartly, fingers extended and joined, palm flat and facing slightly toward the face, thumb along the forefingers, straight line from fingertips to elbow, upper arm horizontal and slightly forward of the body, tip of middle finger touching the right front corner of the headdress (or outside corner of right eyebrow/front corner of glasses if wearing a non-billed hat). Count 2: lower the arm smoothly along the same path, cupping the hand at the waist, returning to attention.
- **Present Arms / Order Arms** — Commands: *Present,* **ARMS** and *Order,* **ARMS**. "Present, ARMS" executes count 1 of the hand salute; "Order, ARMS" executes count 2. (i.e., these two commands split the hand-salute motion across two separate commands rather than one.)

### Marching movements — full table [Chapter 3, paras 3.10–3.19]

| Command | Given on which foot | Execution / count | Key points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Forward, MARCH** (from a halt) | Given at attention/halt (not foot-timed — starts the march) | Step off with the **left foot**, 24-inch step, heel strikes first; coordinated arm swing begins (opposite-arm-opposite-leg, 6" swing forward / 3" swing to rear) | Quick time only from a halt |
| **Flight, HALT** | **Either foot** | Take **one more** 24-inch step, then bring the trailing foot smartly alongside (heels together, 45°) | Can be commanded regardless of which foot is down — a common test trap |
| **Double Time, MARCH** | Either foot (if already marching in quick time) / given at halt to start | From halt: step off left foot, 180 steps/min, 30-inch step, forearms horizontal at waist, hands cupped knuckles out. From quick time: take one more quick-time step, then step off in double time | Only Incline Right/Left, Quick Time MARCH, and Flight HALT may be commanded while in double time |
| **Quick Time, MARCH** (resuming from double time) | Either foot, 4 steps between commands | Take two more double-time steps, then resume quick time | — |
| **Mark Time, MARCH** | **Either foot** (if marching); at halt, no foot-timing needed | If marching: one more 24-inch step, then bring trailing foot level and begin marching in place, feet raised 4 inches, normal arm swing | Executed in quick time only |
| **Forward, MARCH** (resuming from Mark Time) | Given as **left** heel strikes ground | One more step in place, then step off in a full 24-inch step with the left foot | — |
| **Half Step, MARCH** | **Either foot** | One more 24-inch step, then continue in 12-inch steps, heel down first, coordinated arm swing | **Not** executable from a halt; no changes of direction from half step; quick time only |
| **Forward, MARCH** (resuming from Half Step) | Given as **left** heel strikes ground | One more 12-inch step with the right foot, then step out with a full 24-inch step with the left foot | — |
| **Right (Left) Step, MARCH** ("side step") | Given **only from a halt** | Raise trailing leg, 12-inch lateral step (inside heel to inside heel), transfer weight, bring other foot alongside; continued in quick time | Used for short lateral distances only; count 1&3 as the stepping foot strikes, count 2&4 as heels come together |
| **HALT** (from Right/Left Step) | Given as the **heels come together** | Two counts: one more step with the stepping foot, then bring the other foot smartly alongside | — |
| **Change Step, MARCH** | Given as the **right** foot strikes the ground | One more 24-inch step with the left foot; then in one count, tap ball of right foot alongside left heel, shift weight, step off again with the left foot in a 24-inch step | Corrects being out of step with the formation; arm swing briefly suspended during the tap |
| **To the Rear, MARCH** | Given as the **heel of the right foot** strikes the ground | 12-inch step with the left foot (placed in front of/in line with the right foot), pivot **180 degrees** to the right on the balls of both feet (full count, arm swing suspended), then a 12-inch step with the left foot in the new direction, then a full 24-inch step with the right foot | Executed while marching |
| **Right (Left) Flank, MARCH** (flanking movement) | Given as the heel of the **right (left)** foot strikes the ground | One more 24-inch step, pivot **90 degrees** on the ball of the trailing (left/right) foot, then step off in the new direction with a 24-inch step | Used for a quick turn to the right/left over **short distances only**; executed while marching, in a rank/element |
| **Right (Left) Flank, MARCH** ("Face in Marching" — an individual marching alone) | Same command name, given as the corresponding foot strikes the ground | 90-degree pivot on the ball of the **same** foot **and** step off with the other foot **simultaneously**, in **one count** | Distinct technique from the flanking movement above — used when a single individual (e.g., a guide) is marching alone, not as part of a rank |
| **Column Right (Left), MARCH** | Per the general foot rule (para 3.9.2): given as the heel of the **right (left)** foot strikes the ground | Complex, staggered pivot executed element-by-element (see below) | This is a **flight-level formation movement**, not a simple individual pivot — see Column vs. Flanking below |
| **Route Step, MARCH** | Given as either foot strikes the ground (while marching, per para 3.19) | One more 24-inch step, then route step assumed | Neither silence nor cadence required; dress/cover/interval/distance still maintained |
| **At Ease, MARCH** | Given as either foot strikes the ground (while marching) | One more 24-inch step, then at-ease assumed | No cadence; silence, dress, cover, interval, distance still maintained |
| **Eyes, RIGHT (LEFT)** / **Ready, FRONT** | Given on the right (left) foot / left (right) foot respectively | Heads/eyes turn 45 degrees on command; return to front on Ready, FRONT | May be given at a halt or while marching |

### Which movements are executed from the halt vs. while marching

- **Halt only:** Right (Left) Step (side step). Also — practically — Right/Left Face, About Face, and Half Right/Left Face are executed **only from a halt**, at attention [para 3.4].
- **Marching only (not from halt):** Half Step is explicitly stated **not** to be executable from a halt [para 3.13.3]. To the Rear March, Change Step, and the flanking movements are all executed while already marching.
- **Either (from halt or while marching):** Double Time, Mark Time (marches in place either way), Eyes Right/Left.
- **From a halt to start movement:** Forward March, Double Time (initial step-off).

### Column movements vs. flanking movements — the difference [paras 3.17, 4.11]

- **Flanking Movement** (Right/Left Flank, MARCH): a **90-degree turn executed by the entire rank/unit simultaneously** — everyone takes one more step, pivots together, and steps off together in the new direction. Used for **quick, short-distance** direction changes; the whole formation turns "as a block" and each individual's position relative to the others is preserved.
- **Column Movement** (Column Right/Left, MARCH): used for a flight/squadron marching **in column** (elements one behind another) to turn a corner **while preserving column order and reforming abreast**. Each element pivots **in sequence, one at a time, as it reaches the pivot point** — the leading element pivots first and starts half-stepping to let the trailing elements catch up and pivot in turn (the manual describes each successive element taking a longer straight run — 2, 4, then 6 half/full steps at differing pivot geometries — before its own pivot), so the whole flight "wheels" around a fixed pivot point like a door swinging on a hinge, re-establishing dress/cover/interval before resuming Forward, MARCH. This is a substantially more complex, multi-count, staggered maneuver than a flanking movement, and it is normally used at the **flight level** for a formal, precise 90-degree change of direction (e.g., turning a column of flights at a corner), whereas flanking is used for quick individual/short-distance direction changes.

## C) POC-IN-CHARGE / GROUP LEADERSHIP (SOB 8.3)

Source: **AFROTCI 36-2011, Volume 1**, *Cadet's Guide to Leadership Laboratory (LLAB) Curriculum*, memorandum dated **3 September 2024** (the most current publicly available edition located; confirm with detachment cadre whether a newer academic-year edition has since been issued, as this is revised close to annually).
PDF: https://www.usm.edu/aerospace-studies/afrotci36-2011_vol1_llab_curriculum_2024_09_03.pdf

### What POC means — VERIFIED

**POC = Professional Officer Course** — the upper-division AFROTC cadets (normally AS300/AS400, i.e., 3rd- and 4th-year cadets), as distinct from **GMC = General Military Course** (normally AS100/AS200, 1st- and 2nd-year cadets). *(Note: an early automated web-fetch summary during this research incorrectly returned "POC = Point of Contact" — that is wrong and was caught and discarded; the correct, curriculum-verified definition is Professional Officer Course.)*

The curriculum further breaks cadet classification into:
- **Basic Cadet (BC)** — GMC cadets not yet in their POC-selection year (normally AS100).
- **Basic Cadet Leader (BCL)** — GMC cadets in their POC-selection year (normally AS200/AS250/AS500).
- **Intermediate Cadet Leader (ICL)** — cadets who have just entered the POC (normally AS300).
- **Senior Cadet Leader (SCL)** — cadets scheduled to commission the upcoming year (normally AS400).

### POC's role at LLAB — VERIFIED, direct quote [AFROTCI 36-2011, Vol 1, p.4]

> "LLAB-accomplished training occurs at three different levels. First, the General Military Course (GMC) cadets gain knowledge and have an opportunity to carry out activities using practical applications. Second, LLAB gives POC cadets an opportunity to practice their leadership skills to plan, set up, execute, and provide GMCs feedback while executing LLAB. Finally, cadre provide feedback to the POCs on their leadership skills in relation to their plan, set up, execution and feedback provided to GMCs. As a POC, LLAB is your chance to practice your leadership skills that will help you learn and grow into a Second Lieutenant."

### SOB 8.3 exact text — VERIFIED, direct quote [para 8.3]

> **8.3. Sample of Behavior: Demonstrate effective group leadership as the POC-In-Charge.**
> 8.3.1. Requirements: This objective seeks to assess the group leadership skills of project officers in charge of leading, planning, and executing large scale events such as Leadership Laboratory, Physical Training, or special events. ICL cadets should be able to demonstrate effective planning skills, time management, and leadership behaviors to obtain P2. To obtain the P3-level of proficiency, SCL cadets should be highly competent, proven project managers and leaders, and be able to effectively evaluate the performance of subordinate POC using the POC-In-Charge Evaluation Form.
> 8.3.2. References: POC-In-Charge Evaluation Form.
> Instructor/Facilitator: P3-qualified Cadet.

(For context, this sits between SOB 8.2, "Demonstrate effective team leadership" (P1–P3, applying the Full Range Leadership Model), and SOB 8.4, "Demonstrate the principles of an effective debrief.")

### The POC-In-Charge Evaluation Form — VERIFIED, this is the actual current grading rubric [AFROTCI 36-2011 Vol 1, Attachment 1]

The form scores across six graded areas, each rated Ineffective / Satisfactory / Highly Effective:

1. **PLANNING** — considers Cadet Wing deadlines, develops thorough documentation (Operations Orders, trainer info packages, Risk Management plan), gets the plan approved in advance of the event.
2. **COMMUNICATION** — clear written/verbal communication with Cadet Wing and/or Cadre; fosters GMC/POC interaction; appropriately delegates and level-loads tasks among POC cadets.
3. **DECISION-MAKING** — makes clear, timely decisions; uses lessons-learned/continuity/source documents; applies the Risk Management process.
4. **LEADERSHIP** — demonstrates command presence; motivates and directs the Cadet Wing toward mission/objectives; develops and cares for GMC training. Highly Effective standard: "Led by example underpinned in the AF Core Values... Inspired GMC and garnered mission accomplishment."
5. **MISSION** — understands and articulates mission requirements; reinforces leadership/followership skills; completes assigned objectives/Samples of Behavior.
6. **DEBRIEF** — reflects on individual and Cadet Wing performance; develops lessons learned into continuity; conducts an After Action Report (AAR) and produces Action Items (AIs).

### Practical leadership expectations for the cadet in charge

Cross-verified from DAFPAM 34-1203's command-voice guidance (Section 2B, applicable to any cadet giving commands) plus the LLAB curriculum's evaluation criteria above:

- **Voice projection from the diaphragm** [DAFPAM 34-1203, para 2.3.1.1]: "The most important muscle used in breathing is the diaphragm... [it] automatically controls the breath when giving commands." The manual prescribes a specific diaphragm-breathing drill ("Huh and ha," expelled in short puffs using only diaphragm/waist muscles) to build command-voice volume without straining the vocal cords.
- **Positioning**: the commander takes a position in front of, and centered on, the unit, facing it, so the voice reaches everyone [para 2.3.1]; while marching, the leader stays in step with the formation at all times and is at the position of attention while giving commands [para 2.2.1–2.2.2].
- **Snap and command presence**: giving the command of execution at the exact instant the correct foot strikes the ground, standing erect, breathing without effort, speaking clearly [para 2.3.5]. Directly echoed in the POC evaluation's LEADERSHIP category ("Demonstrates command presence").
- **Accountability/reporting**: the DAFPAM's flight/squadron formation procedures show the standard reporting pattern — element leaders report "\_\_\_\_ Element, all present" or "\_\_\_\_ Element, (number) absent" to their flight sergeant, who in turn reports up [para 5.3.4–5.3.5] — this is the model for a POC-in-charge's own accountability reporting up the Cadet Wing chain.
- **Correcting cadets professionally / praise in public, correct in private**: this specific phrase is a widely-taught leadership maxim in AFROTC/military leadership generally, but it is **not found verbatim** in DAFPAM 34-1203 or in the sections of AFROTCI 36-2011 reviewed here — treat it as a general leadership-doctrine principle (commonly taught via the Full Range Leadership Model / AFH 1 curriculum referenced in SOB 8.2) rather than a directly-quotable line from the drill manual. **UNVERIFIED as a direct quote from either source reviewed** — flagged for the cadet to double check against their detachment's specific AS-curriculum leadership text if the exact wording matters for the test.
- **Setting the example**: directly stated in the POC-In-Charge Evaluation Form's Highly Effective LEADERSHIP standard: "Led by example underpinned in the AF Core Values."

### AFROTC Cadet Wing organizational structure — how a POC-in-Charge fits

**Caveat**: the org chart below is presented in AFROTCI 36-2011 Vol 1 explicitly as a **sample/example** "000th Cadet Wing" structure for illustration — exact position titles and structure vary somewhat by detachment. Verified example structure:

- **Cadet Wing Commander (CW/CC)** — directs and is responsible for all actions within the Cadet Wing; reports to the Operations Flight Commander (an active-duty cadre officer); leads both POC and GMC in accomplishing the detachment mission.
- **Cadet Wing Deputy Commander (CW/CD)** — advises/supports the CW/CC; assumes command in CW/CC's absence; supervises training and staff performance.
- Subordinate groups/squadrons in the sample structure include a **Training Readiness Group (TRG)** (with a Basic Cadet Squadron and Basic Cadet Leader Squadron under it), an **Operations Group (OG)** (Physical Fitness Squadron, Operations Support Squadron, Honor Guard Squadron), and a **Mission Support Group (MSG)** (Force Support Squadron, Public Affairs, Recruiting).

A **POC-in-Charge** (of a given LLAB event, PT session, or special event) is a **project-officer role** layered on top of this permanent staff structure — a POC cadet (often an ICL, sometimes supervised/evaluated by an SCL) is assigned as the officer-in-charge of planning and executing a specific Practical Military Training (PMT) event (LLAB, PT, Field Training prep, etc.), and is graded using the POC-In-Charge Evaluation Form described above. This is a temporary/event-based leadership position, distinct from (though often held concurrently with) a cadet's permanent Cadet Wing staff billet.

## Most Likely Test Questions (highest-yield facts)

1. **Quick time cadence: 100–120 steps/minute.** Double time: **180 steps/minute.**
2. **Forward march step: 24 inches.** Half step: **12 inches.** Right/Left (side) step: **12 inches**, from a halt only. Double time step: **30 inches.** "Steps Backward": 12 inches (not called "back step" in the current manual).
3. **Position of attention: feet form a 45-degree angle**; knees straight but **not locked**; weight equally on heels and balls of both feet; thumbs along trouser seam.
4. **Rest position distinctions (very commonly tested):**
   - *Parade Rest*: left foot moves 12" left, hands form an "X" behind the back, **silent**.
   - *At Ease*: **right foot stays in place**, position unchanged, **silent**.
   - *Rest*: same as At Ease (right foot stays put) but **talking (moderate speech) is allowed**.
   - *Fall Out*: may leave ranks/position entirely, talking allowed.
   - Memory hook: At Ease and Rest are physically identical — only difference is silence vs. speech allowed.
5. **Preparatory command / command of execution**: preparatory command has rising inflection; command of execution has no inflection but higher pitch than the preparatory command; ideal interval is one step between them (three steps for squadron+ size units); both given on the foot matching the direction of the movement, EXCEPT Halt, which can be given on **either** foot.
6. **"AS YOU WERE"** revokes a command **before** the command of execution/movement begins; if the movement has already started, use follow-on commands instead; if a command is given wrong, troops execute it as best they can.
7. **Combined commands**: FALL IN, AT EASE, REST (single utterance, no separate preparatory/execution parts).
8. **Facing movements** (Right/Left Face, About Face) are done from a **halt only**, in **quick time**, **2 counts**; Right/Left Face pivots 90°, About Face pivots 180°.
9. **To the Rear March**: preparatory command given on the **right foot**; 12-inch step with left foot, 180° pivot, then continue.
10. **Change Step**: given on the **right foot**; corrects being out of step.
11. **Column movements happen element-by-element (staggered pivots) to preserve column order**, unlike **flanking movements**, which turn the whole rank **simultaneously and immediately** for short quick turns.
12. **POC = Professional Officer Course** (AS300/AS400 cadets), **GMC = General Military Course** (AS100/AS200 cadets). Do not confuse POC with "point of contact."
13. **The drill manual is now DAFPAM 34-1203 (13 Sep 2022)**, not AFMAN/DAFMAN 36-2203 — it superseded that manual.
14. **POC-In-Charge Evaluation Form** grades: Planning, Communication, Decision-Making, Leadership, Mission, Debrief.

### Flagged conflicts / uncertainties

1. **Task premise used the wrong document number.** The task asked to verify against "DAFMAN 36-2203" — that publication was renumbered/superseded by **DAFPAM 34-1203** on 13 September 2022. This is flagged prominently above. If the cadet's official grading checklist/test bank cites "AFMAN 36-2203," that is likely just an outdated label carried over in study materials — the content should still track DAFPAM 34-1203 closely, but the cadet should confirm with cadre which document their specific test cites.
2. **"Back step" is not current AF terminology.** The current manual's equivalent (12-inch steps backward, left foot first, ≤4 steps) is called "\_\_\_\_ Steps Backward, MARCH" and appears only in the context of positioning a forming squadron — it is not part of the individual/flight marching-movement sequence (Chapter 3) the way Half Step or Right Step are. If a test question asks for "back step" length expecting 12 inches, that number is very likely still correct, but flag the terminology mismatch if precision matters.
3. **SOB numbering scope mismatch.** The current AFROTC curriculum (AFROTCI 36-2011 Vol 1) defines **SOB 7.2 as stationary-only** (Attention/Parade Rest, Right/Left Face, About Face, Present/Order Arms) and places all marching movements under **SOB 7.3** ("Demonstrate basic drill of the flight"). The task's description of 7.2 included marching movements; those are covered fully above regardless, but be aware of this possible SOB-boundary discrepancy if the grading rubric is strict about which SOB number a given movement falls under.
4. **"Praise in public, correct in private" is not a verbatim quote** from either DAFPAM 34-1203 or the AFROTCI 36-2011 sections reviewed — it's a well-established leadership-doctrine principle but should be treated as general knowledge, not a citable quote from these two specific publications, unless the cadet finds it explicitly in their AS-curriculum leadership textbook (AFH 1 or the Full Range Leadership Model material), which was outside the scope of what was directly reviewed here.
5. **Column Right/Left "which foot" for the preparatory command** is not explicitly restated in Chapter 4's Column Movements section — it is inferred from the general rule in para 3.9.2 (preparatory command/command of execution given on the foot matching the direction of the turn). This is a reasonable, standard inference consistent with how every other directional command in the manual works, but it is not a direct quote specific to Column Right/Left the way it is for, e.g., To the Rear March or Change Step.
6. **AFROTCI 36-2011 currency**: the version reviewed carries a "3 September 2024" introductory memo. AFROTC curriculum documents are revised close to annually; confirm with the cadet's own detachment cadre whether a newer edition (e.g., for AY 2025-2026) has superseded this one — no newer public copy was found during this research.
