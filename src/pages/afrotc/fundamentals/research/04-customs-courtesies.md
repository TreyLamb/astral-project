## 4.2 SALUTING — highest-yield section

### A. Exact mechanics of the hand salute

From AFMAN 36-2203, *Drill and Ceremonies* (confirmed verbatim across AFROTC detachment manuals that quote it directly, e.g., AFROTC Det 025 Cadet Manual):

1. **Command:** "Present, ARMS" (pronounced "Harms"). Individual salute is rendered on your own initiative (no command needed) when greeting/being greeted.
2. **Raise the right hand smartly**, in the most direct manner, while extending and joining the fingers.
3. **Hand:** flat, fingers extended and joined, thumb along the forefinger (not tucked, not spread) — the palm forms a straight, unbroken line from fingertip to elbow. **No cupping** at the top of the salute.
4. **Palm:** tilted slightly toward the face (not flat toward the ground, not facing outward).
5. **Upper arm:** horizontal, slightly forward of the body, parallel to the ground.
6. **Forearm/hand placement:**
   - If wearing a **brimmed/billed headdress** (e.g., service cap, flight cap with visor): tip of the **middle finger touches the right front corner of the headdress** (the edge of the brim nearest the right eyebrow).
   - If wearing a **non-billed hat** (garrison/flight cap) **or no headgear** (indoors, reporting): middle finger touches the **outer corner of the right eyebrow**, or the front corner of eyeglasses if worn.
7. **Return (count 2):** bring the arm smoothly and smartly downward, **retracing the same path** used to raise it; **cup the hand as it passes the waist**, returning to the position of attention. (Note: hand is cupped in the *position of attention*, not during the salute itself — cupping only reappears as the hand drops past the waist on the way down.)
8. Body remains at the position of attention throughout; only the arm moves.

**Common test traps:** "cupped hand" is correct only at *attention* and while lowering past the waist — the salute itself is a flat hand. The salute is **not** a wave, and the wrist must stay straight (unbroken line from elbow to fingertip).

### B. WHO to salute

Render the salute to:
- **All commissioned and warrant officers** of the U.S. Armed Forces (superior in grade)
- Commissioned officers of **friendly foreign nations**
- The **President** of the United States (Commander in Chief), the **Vice President**, **Secretary of Defense**, service **Secretaries**
- **Medal of Honor recipients** — regardless of their rank (this is a unique, formally codified Air Force custom: even senior officers salute a Medal of Honor recipient first, and members are encouraged to salute MOH recipients whenever the medal is worn, even in civilian clothes)

**Who is NOT saluted:**
- Enlisted members do not salute each other; NCOs do not receive salutes as a matter of course
- **Cadets do not salute other cadets of equal or lesser grade** — cadet officers/cadre are saluted according to detachment protocol, but general cadet-to-cadet salutes are not the norm outside specific reporting/military-courtesy contexts
- Civilians (except the President as Commander in Chief)

### C. WHEN to salute — and WHEN NOT to

**General outdoor rule:** In uniform, outdoors, salute all officers you recognize as such, whether on or off a military installation, as soon as they are recognized (approximately 6 paces is the commonly cited distance to raise the salute so it can be seen and returned; hold until it is returned or the officer is about 6 paces past).

**Do NOT salute:**
- **Indoors** — except (a) when formally **reporting** to an officer, or (b) during a **ceremony/formal occasion** (e.g., presenting the report, indoor honors, or under-arms situations described below).
- **In formation** — only the person in charge (commander) of the formation salutes and/or renders the greeting on behalf of the formation; individual members do not salute out of ranks.
- **On work details** — the individual actually working does not have to stop and salute; if in a work detail, only the person in charge salutes.
- **When carrying articles in both hands** (rendering a salute impractical) — greet verbally instead. If carrying one item, carry it in the **left** hand so the right hand is free to salute.
- **In public conveyances** (buses, aircraft cabins) **or crowded public places** (sporting events, theaters) where a salute would be impractical or draw undue attention.
- **While driving a vehicle** — a salute is not rendered by the driver of a moving vehicle (unsafe); a courteous nod/greeting is acceptable. (Passengers who are not driving may salute.)
- **In combat/simulated-combat or field conditions** where saluting could expose an officer as a target, or where directed as a **"no-salute" area/zone**.
- **During physical training** — saluting while in PT gear is authorized but not required.
- When **the senior's hands are full** — the junior still renders the greeting (and, per some detachment manuals, still salutes if their own hands are free); the senior is not obligated to return a salute if their hands are full, but should return the verbal greeting.

**Distance/timing:**
- Initiate the salute far enough out (~6 paces) that it can be clearly seen and returned before passing.
- Hold the salute until it is returned, or (for a person/colors passing you) until they are approximately 6 paces beyond you.

### D. Who initiates, who terminates, and the verbal greeting

- The **junior member always initiates** the salute; the senior returns it.
- The **salute is normally accompanied by an oral greeting** — e.g., "Good morning, Sir" / "Good morning, Ma'am" (adjusted for time of day: morning/afternoon/evening).
- The **senior officer terminates** by returning the salute; the junior then drops the salute (in the reporting sequence specifically, the reporting cadet holds the salute until the officer returns it, then drops it).
- In a **formation**, the formation leader/commander renders the salute and the greeting on behalf of the whole formation; individuals in ranks do not salute separately.

### E. Saluting the flag / colors, National Anthem, Reveille, Retreat, To the Colors

- **Reveille** (raising the flag, typically at the start of the duty day, commonly 0600–0700 depending on installation) is preceded by/associated with a bugle call; personnel in uniform outdoors **stand at parade rest** during the initial Reveille call, then come to **attention and salute** when "To the Colors" (or the National Anthem, if played) sounds as the flag is actually raised.
- **Retreat** (lowering the flag, typically at end of duty day, e.g., 1630–1700) — same sequence: parade rest during the Retreat bugle call, then **attention + salute** during "To the Colors"/National Anthem as the flag is lowered.
- Face the flag if visible; if not visible, **face the direction of the music/sound**.
- **Begin the salute at the first note** and **hold until the last note** of the National Anthem or To the Colors.
- **In a vehicle:** pull over, stop, and sit quietly at attention (occupants do not have to exit or salute from inside a moving/parked vehicle) until the music ends or the flag reaches full height/is fully lowered.
- **Civilians and military members out of uniform:** stand at attention, face the flag/music, and place the right hand over the heart (no salute).

### F. Formation vs. not-in-formation rule (the master rule)

- **In formation:** only the formation leader salutes/renders honors on the unit's behalf; individual members remain at attention (or whatever position ordered) and do not salute individually.
- **Not in formation (individual):** the general who/when/where rules above apply per person.

### G. Vehicle salute / staff car / flag plate rules

- The **driver of a moving vehicle does not salute** (safety).
- **Passengers** in a vehicle may salute an officer passed on the roadway if practical.
- Vehicles displaying **general/flag officer plates** (a vehicle placard denoting a general officer or equivalent) are traditionally saluted by sentries/gate guards as they pass, since the flag/plate itself signifies the officer is aboard — this is a sentry/gate-guard custom more than a general cadet requirement, but is commonly tested trivia.
- At Reveille/Retreat, vehicles **stop and occupants sit at attention** (see above) rather than salute from the vehicle.

## 4.3 REPORTING IN / REPORTING OUT

*(Note: exact phrasing/step order can vary slightly by detachment; the sequence below is drawn directly from multiple current AFROTC detachment training manuals, including the AFROTC Det 025 (Arizona State University) Cadet Manual, and is consistent with the general AFROTC standard taught at Field Training.)*

### Reporting In — step by step

1. **Approach the door.** If closed, **knock once, firmly**. If open, knock once on the door frame. If there's no response, wait ~10 seconds and knock again.
2. Wait for **"Enter."**
3. Enter, and if the door was closed, **shut it behind you without turning your back to the room** (i.e., without facing the door while you close it), unless told otherwise.
4. **March by the most direct route** to a position approximately **two paces in front of the officer's desk** (or one pace in front of the chair, if a chair is the reporting point). Come to the **position of attention**, eyes 15° above the horizon.
5. **Render a proper salute** and say:

   > **"Sir/Ma'am, Cadet [Last Name] reports as ordered."**

   *(Use "reports as ordered" only if you were specifically told/ordered to report. If you are reporting on your own initiative — not summoned — drop "as ordered" and simply say: "Sir/Ma'am, Cadet [Last Name] reports.")*
6. The officer **returns the salute**, then typically says something like **"Be seated"** or gives further instruction.
7. Sit (or remain) at the **position of attention** until told **"At ease"** (or given other instruction). You may be told to remain standing at attention or parade rest instead of sitting, depending on the situation.
8. **Headgear:** Remove headgear indoors as a matter of course (indoor policy — see 4.7) *except* that during the act of reporting, you still render the hand salute even indoors — this is the **specific, named exception** to the "no salute indoors" rule. If you are wearing headgear (e.g., because you just came from outdoors and haven't removed it yet), the salute is still executed as normal against the headgear; if bareheaded, salute against the eyebrow.

### Reporting Out — step by step

1. When you sense the meeting concluding, ask: **"Will that be all?"**
   - If the officer says **"Yes, that will be all"** → proceed to depart.
   - If the officer says **"No, that will not be all"** → the meeting continues; do not ask again until there's another natural lull.
   - If the officer has already said **"That will be all"** (or equivalent) **unprompted**, do **NOT** ask "Will that be all?" — that phrase has already answered the question.
2. Stand up to the **position of attention**.
3. Take **one step to the side** (left or right, whichever fits the room) and **one step to the rear** (with coordinated rear-step arm swing).
4. **Render a salute** and say:

   > **"Good morning/afternoon/evening, [Rank and Name]."**

5. Once the officer **returns the salute and greeting**, **drop your salute** and take the most direct route out of the office.
6. If the door was shut, open it, exit, and **shut it behind you** unless told otherwise.

**Verbatim scripts a cadet should memorize:**
- Reporting in (ordered): *"Sir/Ma'am, Cadet [Last Name] reports as ordered."*
- Reporting in (self-initiated): *"Sir/Ma'am, Cadet [Last Name] reports."*
- Reporting out: *"Will that be all?"* → then *"Good morning/afternoon/evening, [Rank/Name]."*

## 4.1 / 4.4 CUSTOMS & COURTESIES — GENERAL

### Walking, riding, and boarding order

- A **junior always walks on the left** of, and normally slightly behind, a senior — the senior takes the position of honor on the right (a tradition rooted in swordsmanship, so the senior's right/sword arm was unobstructed).
- **Exception:** during a uniform/troop inspection, the **senior walks on the left** of the person being inspected/of the formation, so they have the best vantage point.
- **Entering/exiting vehicles (car, boat) and aircraft:** the rule commonly summarized is **"juniors in first, seniors out first"** — i.e., the **junior boards first**, the **senior boards last**; when disembarking, the **senior exits first**, juniors last. Seniors generally occupy the seat farthest to the rear/aft as the position of honor.
  - **FLAG:** This "junior in first / senior out first" convention is the version most consistently reported across sources, but it is **not codified in a single, universally-cited AFROTC regulation** the way saluting mechanics are — treat it as a well-established customary courtesy rather than a black-letter regulatory citation. Some sources describe it in the context of boats/watercraft specifically (Navy/Navy-derived custom) and generalize it to cars/aircraft. If your detachment's test references a specific publication for this rule, confirm the citation with your cadre.

### Calling a room to attention / "Carry on"

- When a senior officer enters a room, the **first cadet to notice** the officer calls **"Room, ATTENTION"** (or "[Room/Area], STAND BY" then "ATTENTION").
- **Exception:** if a **more senior (or equally/more relevant-ranking) officer is already present** in the room, the room is generally **not** called to attention again for a subsequently-arriving officer of equal or lesser seniority — attention is called anew only if the newly arriving individual **outranks** whoever is already the senior person present.
- After being called to attention, the senior officer may direct **"Carry on"** (or "As you were," "Take seats," etc.), at which point the room relaxes/returns to its prior activity/seated position.
- If the senior officer entering is enlisted (cadre/NCO) rather than commissioned, some detachments only call **"Room/Area, STAND BY"** without going to full attention — verify against your specific detachment's practice, as this varies.

### Titles of address

- **Commissioned officers:** address as **rank + last name** (preferred, e.g., "Good morning, Lieutenant Colonel Sterling"), or simply **"Sir"/"Ma'am."**
- **NCOs (enlisted cadre):** address by **rank (+ last name)**; NCOs are **not** addressed as "Sir" or "Ma'am."
- **Cadets:** addressed as **"Cadet [Last Name]."** A cadet officer (POC in a leadership billet) may be addressed by rank + last name, or as "Sir"/"Ma'am," per detachment custom.
- The GMC/AFROTC "7 Basic Responses" bracket every reply to cadre with "Sir/Ma'am" leading or trailing: *"Yes, Sir/Ma'am." "No, Sir/Ma'am." "No excuse, Sir/Ma'am." "Sir/Ma'am, I do not understand." "Sir/Ma'am, I do not know." "Sir/Ma'am, may I make a statement?" "Sir/Ma'am, may I ask a question?"*

### Dining-in / Dining-out / mess etiquette

- **Dining-in** is a formal military function for members of the unit only (mess night); **dining-out** is the same format opened up to spouses/guests, typically with military traditions softened for civilian guests.
- **Dress:** mess dress for officers; enlisted/cadets wear mess dress or semi-formal equivalent as directed.
- Core "Rules of the Mess" customs frequently tested:
  - Arrive **no later than 10 minutes before** the dinner is announced.
  - Do not bring drinks or lit smoking materials into the dining room; stand quietly behind your chair until seated is directed.
  - **No one leaves the mess while it is convened** except in a genuine emergency ("military protocol overrides calls of nature" is the traditional joking phrasing, but the real rule is you don't leave without excusing yourself through the President of the Mess/Vice).
  - Participate in **all toasts** unless you personally (or your group) are the one being toasted.
  - Infractions of mess etiquette are typically punished informally (a "grog bowl" fine), not punitively.
- Everyday dining-facility (chow hall) courtesies: remove headgear indoors before/while eating (except under-arms exceptions), don't cut in line, junior members typically let seniors go first in line as a courtesy (not a hard rule), be quiet/orderly, table manners matter as they reflect officer-candidate standards.

### Telephone / email courtesies

- **Answer/identify** with unit and your name/rank when on official business (e.g., "Detachment 025, Cadet [Name] speaking, how may I help you, Sir/Ma'am?").
- Maintain the same **"Sir/Ma'am"** courtesy verbally on the phone as in person.
- **Email:** use proper military/professional formatting, correct rank and titles in salutations, proofread, and use the same level of respect and formality as in-person communication with cadre/officers. Avoid text-speak/casual tone with cadre.

## 4.6 OUTDOOR CEREMONIES

### Reveille and Retreat

- **Reveille** marks the **raising** of the flag/start of the duty day. **Retreat** marks the **end** of the duty day and precedes the **lowering** of the flag; the actual lowering is accompanied by **"To the Colors"** (or the National Anthem).
- **Sequence of music:** during Reveille/Retreat there is first a bugle call (Reveille or Retreat) during which personnel stand at **parade rest**; this is followed by **"To the Colors"** (or the National Anthem) during the actual raising/lowering of the flag, at which point personnel **come to attention and salute**.
- Exact clock times vary by installation/detachment and are typically posted locally (commonly early morning, e.g., ~0600–0700 for Reveille, and late afternoon, e.g., ~1630–1700 for Retreat) — **confirm your specific detachment's posted times**, since this is installation-specific and not fixed by regulation to a single time.

**What to do, by situation:**

| Situation | Reveille/Retreat bugle call | To the Colors / National Anthem |
|---|---|---|
| In uniform, outdoors, not in formation | Stop, stand at **parade rest**, face the flag (or the music if flag not visible) | Come to **attention**, **salute**, hold until last note |
| In civilian clothes, outdoors | Stop, stand at **attention**, face the flag/music | Stand at attention, **right hand over heart** (no salute) |
| In a vehicle | Vehicle should **pull over and stop** | Occupants **sit quietly at attention** in the vehicle until music ends/flag fully raised or lowered — no need to exit |
| In formation | Formation stands at parade rest, then attention | Only the **formation commander salutes**; formation remains at attention |
| Alone / individual | Same as "not in formation" above | Same as above |

### National Anthem outdoors

- **Face the flag** if it is visible and displayed; if it is not visible, **face the direction of the music/sound source**.
- Come to (or remain at) the **position of attention**.
- **Uniformed members salute** at the **first note** and hold the salute until the **last note**.
- **Non-uniformed/civilian:** attention + right hand over the heart, no salute.

### Pledge of Allegiance outdoors — uniformed vs. civilian (VERIFY / commonly misreported)

Per **4 U.S.C. § 4** (the federal flag code section governing the Pledge):
- Persons **in military uniform** should **stand at attention, face the flag, remain silent, and render the military salute**.
- Persons **not in uniform** (including off-duty veterans, since a 2013 amendment, Public Law 113-66) render the Pledge the traditional civilian way: standing at attention facing the flag, right hand over the heart (men not in uniform also remove non-religious headdress).
- **So: yes — a cadet in uniform outdoors salutes during the Pledge of Allegiance** (does not recite it aloud; remains silent) — this is the codified federal rule and matches Air Force protocol guidance.

**FLAG — possible area of confusion:** Some detachment-level and secondary sources describe uniformed members as simply standing at attention (no salute) during the Pledge specifically, reserving the salute for the National Anthem/To the Colors only, and note that when a formation/group is mostly civilians, reciting the Pledge is treated as optional for the uniformed member present (who still stands silently at attention). **The safest, most defensible answer based on the primary federal statute (4 U.S.C. § 4) is: in uniform + outdoors = salute during the Pledge, remain silent, face the flag.** If your detachment's test material specifies otherwise, defer to your instructor's guidance, as this is one of the most commonly-inconsistent points across secondary sources.

### Parades and passing colors — the six-paces rule

- When the **uncased national color (flag)** approaches, or when you are passing it, **render the salute when the colors are six paces from you**, and **hold the salute until the colors are six paces past you**.
- During "Eyes Right" at a pass-in-review, individual marchers in the passing formation turn their eyes toward the reviewing officer/colors on command, while the formation commander salutes on behalf of the unit.

### Funerals / military honors — basics

- Full military honors typically include a **flag-draped casket**, a **firing party** (rifle volley), **"Taps,"** and the **folding and presentation of the flag** to next of kin.
- Attendees in uniform render the **hand salute** during the folding/presentation of the flag and during "Taps," consistent with saluting-the-flag protocol; civilians place a hand over the heart.
- Pallbearers and firing-party details follow specific drill sequences beyond the scope of routine cadet testing; know the general elements (flag, volley, Taps, flag presentation) as baseline knowledge.

## 4.7 INDOOR CEREMONIES

### National Anthem indoors

- Military members in uniform **come to attention** at the first note; **face the flag if one is displayed indoors, or the music if not.**
- **In formation, wearing headgear:** render the **military salute** at the first note, hold until the last note (this is the standard "in formation, under arms/with headgear" case).
- **Not wearing headgear** (bareheaded, whether in formation or not): stand at **attention only — do NOT salute** — hold that position until the last note.
- **Exception — "under arms":** a member in uniform who is **under arms** (e.g., carrying a weapon, such as on a color guard or armed detail) **does salute**, even indoors and even without headgear, per Air Force protocol guidance.
- **Headgear:** if you are wearing authorized headgear indoors (e.g., because you're part of a detail/formation that retains headgear indoors), keep it on for the salute; otherwise headgear is normally removed indoors (see general indoor headgear rule below).
- **Civilians / out of uniform:** attention, right hand over the heart.

### Pledge of Allegiance indoors — uniformed members (VERIFY)

- Consistent with 4 U.S.C. § 4: uniformed members **stand at attention, face the flag, remain silent, and salute** — the same rule applies indoors as outdoors under the federal statute; being indoors does not itself change the uniformed member's obligation under the flag code.
- **Common practical nuance cited in JROTC/ROTC guidance:** when the assembled group is predominantly civilians (e.g., a school assembly) and the Pledge is being recited as a civilian activity, a uniformed cadet/member present is typically still expected to stand at attention and **may salute** per the federal code, while reciting is optional/silent for the uniformed member specifically. **This point has some inconsistency across secondary sources** — treat "stand at attention, face the flag, remain silent, salute" as the codified default, and flag for your instructor if your unit teaches a variant.

### Room-attention protocol indoors (recap from 4.4)

- First cadet to notice a senior/officer entering calls **"Room, ATTENTION."**
- The senior officer may then direct **"Carry on"/"As you were"/"Take seats."**
- No repeated call to attention if an equal-or-more-senior officer is already present in the room (see 4.4 above).

### Formal indoor dinners

- Follow **dining-in/dining-out** etiquette (see 4.4): stand behind your chair until seated is announced, no drinks/smoking materials before being seated, participate in toasts, do not leave while the mess is convened without proper excusal.

### When to remove headgear indoors — and the exceptions

**General rule:** remove headgear **indoors**.

**Exceptions — headgear stays ON indoors when:**
1. **"Under arms"** — armed with a weapon (e.g., on guard duty, a color guard detail, or carrying a firearm as part of official duty).
2. Performing **official duty in formation** as a member of an armed guard/detail, or other duty specifically requiring headgear indoors (e.g., certain drill/ceremony details).
3. Inside a **chapel** while functioning as an armed guard or during certain military honors — note that for general worship attendance, the *general* civilian/military custom is actually to remove headgear in a chapel/place of worship (this is the opposite direction from "under arms," so don't conflate the two — under-arms/duty status is the reason headgear stays on, not the location itself).
4. Passing **through** a building quickly (transiting a lobby, etc.) is sometimes treated with more latitude than being stationed indoors, but the safe default for testing purposes is: **indoors = headgear off**, with the **under-arms/official-armed-duty exception** being the one most commonly tested.

## Most Likely Test Questions

1. **"What is the correct hand/finger position for a salute, and where does the middle finger touch?"** — Flat hand, fingers and thumb joined/extended, straight line from elbow to fingertips, palm tilted slightly inward; middle finger touches the **right front corner of the headdress** (billed cap) or the **outer corner of the right eyebrow** (non-billed cap/bareheaded).
2. **"Who salutes first?"** — The **junior** member always initiates the salute.
3. **"Do you salute indoors?"** — **No**, except when **formally reporting** to an officer, during **ceremonies**, or when **under arms**.
4. **"Who do you NOT salute?"** — Enlisted personnel, other cadets of equal/lesser grade, civilians (except the President).
5. **"What is the exception for Medal of Honor recipients?"** — They are saluted **regardless of rank**; it's a formally recognized Air Force custom (unusual because it can mean a senior officer salutes first).
6. **"When does a driver of a vehicle salute?"** — **Never** while actively driving; pull over and sit at attention during Reveille/Retreat music instead.
7. **"What do you say when reporting to an officer who summoned you?"** — *"Sir/Ma'am, Cadet [Last Name] reports as ordered."*
8. **"What's different if you report on your own initiative (not ordered)?"** — Drop "as ordered": *"Sir/Ma'am, Cadet [Last Name] reports."*
9. **"What is the reporting-out procedure?"** — Ask *"Will that be all?"*, step side + back with arm swing, salute, say *"Good morning/afternoon/evening, [Rank/Name],"* drop salute once returned, exit.
10. **"Who walks on the senior's right?"** — The **senior**; juniors walk on the **left** (except during inspections, when the senior walks on the left of the person/formation being inspected).
11. **"Who boards a vehicle/aircraft first? Who exits first?"** — **Juniors board first; seniors exit first** (juniors in, seniors out).
12. **"During the National Anthem, when do you start/stop saluting?"** — Start at the **first note**, hold through the **last note**.
13. **"What's the six-paces rule?"** — Salute the uncased national colors when they are **six paces** away and hold until they are **six paces past** you.
14. **"Does a uniformed member salute during the Pledge of Allegiance?"** — **Yes**, per 4 U.S.C. § 4 — stand at attention, face the flag, remain silent, and salute (do not recite aloud).
15. **"Do you salute indoors during the National Anthem?"** — Only if **under arms**, or wearing headgear as part of a formation; **otherwise, stand at attention without saluting.**
16. **"What is 'Room, Attention' and who calls it, and when is it skipped?"** — First cadet who notices a senior officer entering calls it; skipped if an equal/more senior officer is already present.
17. **"When is saluting excused/impractical?"** — Both hands full, in formation (only the leader salutes), driving, in crowds/public conveyances, on a work detail, in a designated no-salute area, in combat/field conditions.

## Confidence & Sources

**High confidence (directly corroborated by AFMAN 36-2203 salute-mechanics language, quoted verbatim in current AFROTC detachment training manuals):**
- Salute mechanics (hand/arm position, fingertip placement, no cupping during the salute, cupping on return past the waist).
- Reporting-in and reporting-out scripts and sequence (knock, enter, position at desk, salute, "reports as ordered," seated/at ease, "will that be all?" reporting-out sequence).
- Reveille/Retreat sequence (parade rest during bugle call → attention/salute during To the Colors/Anthem), vehicle-stop rule.
- Six-paces rule for saluting the colors.
- Junior initiates/senior returns/terminates the salute; verbal greeting accompanies the salute.
- Formation salute rule (only the formation leader salutes).
- Medal of Honor salute-regardless-of-rank custom.

**Medium confidence (well-supported by multiple secondary/detachment sources but not independently verified against the exact current DAFI 90-1201/AFH 1 paragraph numbers in this session):**
- Vehicle "juniors in first, seniors out first" convention — strongly and consistently reported, but I could not pull the exact current publication paragraph citing it; treat as a settled customary rule but flag if your test wants a specific regulation citation.
- Walking on the senior's left (and the inspection exception).
- Room-attention "senior already present" exception — corroborated but drawn from a general web summary rather than a directly quoted regulation excerpt in this research pass.
- Titles-of-address conventions for NCOs vs. officers vs. cadets.
- Dining-in/dining-out etiquette specifics (arrival time, no drinks before seated, toasting rules) — corroborated across several AFROTC/Air Force sources but with some wording variation.

**Flagged as uncertain / where sources conflict — verify with your cadre before the test:**
1. **Pledge of Allegiance salute rule (outdoors and indoors).** The federal statute, 4 U.S.C. § 4, is unambiguous: uniformed members stand at attention, face the flag, remain silent, and **salute**. However, several secondary AFJROTC/ROTC study-guide pages describe uniformed members as merely standing at attention (no salute) during the Pledge, reserving the salute specifically for the National Anthem/Colors, particularly when the setting is a predominantly-civilian assembly. **This document defaults to the codified federal rule (salute)** but flags it because this is one of the most commonly-misreported points in ROTC study materials — confirm with your detachment instructor which version your unit's test expects.
2. **Vehicle boarding/exiting order citation.** The "junior in first, senior out first" rule is consistently reported across sources but most clearly documented in Navy/naval-tradition contexts and generalized elsewhere; I did not find a directly-quoted Air Force regulatory paragraph for it in this research pass. Confirm the exact source your course cites if the test asks for a regulation number.
3. **Exact Reveille/Retreat clock times.** These are installation/detachment-specific and not fixed by a single Air Force-wide regulation to a specific hour — do not assume a single "correct" time unless your detachment's schedule specifies one.
4. **"Room, Attention" exception details** and whether an enlisted cadre member arriving warrants a full call to attention — this varies by detachment (one detachment's manual specifies only "Stand By" for an arriving enlisted member, not full attention); confirm local practice.
5. I was unable to retrieve clean, directly-quotable text from the current **DAFI 90-1201** (the 2024 successor to AFI 34-1201, Protocol) or from **AFH 1** itself during this research pass (PDF text-extraction failures); the salute-mechanics and drill-specific content above is corroborated via **AFMAN 36-2203** language as reproduced in current AFROTC detachment manuals, which is the correct authoritative source for drill/salute mechanics specifically. If your course explicitly tests a numbered paragraph citation from DAFI 90-1201/AFH 1, verify directly with your cadre or the e-publishing site.
