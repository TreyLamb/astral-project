## 2.1 — Current DAF Chain of Command (detail)

### 2. Secretary of Defense
**Secretary of Defense** — The Honorable Pete Hegseth (29th Secretary of Defense)
Sworn in 25 January 2025.

**⚠️ CRITICAL — the "Department of War" question. Here is the correct answer as of 3 Sep 2026:**

- The **statutory, legal name is still the Department of Defense**, and the statutory title is still
  **Secretary of Defense**. Only Congress can change it, and Congress has not yet done so.
- **Executive Order 14347, "Restoring the United States Department of War"** (signed 5 September 2025)
  authorized **"Department of War"** and **"Secretary of War"** as **secondary titles** for use in
  non-statutory contexts. The Pentagon and many officials use them routinely in public communications,
  press releases, and signage.
- The **FY2026 NDAA (P.L. 119-60, signed 18 December 2025) did NOT enact the renaming.**
- During 2026 the House and Senate Armed Services Committees and House Appropriations advanced language
  to make the change statutory (a rename would require amending roughly 7,600 references in federal law;
  CBO scored the cost in January 2026). The House passed a rename provision in its FY2027 NDAA vote.
  **As of 3 September 2026 that has not been signed into law.**

**What a cadet should say:** *"Secretary of Defense — the Honorable Pete Hegseth."* If asked about the
Department of War, add: *"'Department of War' and 'Secretary of War' are secondary titles authorized by
Executive Order 14347 in September 2025; the statutory name remains the Department of Defense."*
That answer is correct and complete regardless of which convention your instructor prefers.
If your detachment's materials use "Secretary of War," say that and note the statutory name — you will
not be wrong either way.

**Teaching point:** The Chairman is the **principal military advisor** to the President, the Secretary of
Defense, and the National Security Council. He is **NOT in the operational chain of command.** The
operational chain runs **President → Secretary of Defense → Combatant Commanders.** The service chain
(the one a cadet recites) runs **President → SecDef → SecAF → CSAF/CSO**. Instructors like this
distinction — know it.

AETC comprises the Air Force Accessions Center, two Numbered Air Forces, and Air University; roughly

**Commander, Air Force Accessions Center (AFAC) and Commander, Air Force Recruiting Service (AFRS)**

**Commander, Jeanne M. Holm Center for Officer Accessions and Citizen Development**
— Brigadier General Christopher T. Lay

**Commander, Headquarters Air Force ROTC** — Colonel Michael G. Fleming
Assumed command **29 June 2026** at Maxwell AFB, AL. Col. Matthew E. Tipton, Deputy Commander of the

HQ AFROTC oversees the program at more than 1,100 colleges and universities.

### Detachment and cadet wing structure (generic — fill in your own names)

Below HQ AFROTC, the structure is local. The generic model:

- **Detachment Commander (Det/CC)** — an active-duty Colonel or Lieutenant Colonel; also serves as
  Professor of Aerospace Studies (PAS). This is the cadet's senior rater and the first commissioned
  officer in the cadet's direct chain.
- **Operations Flight Commander / Recruiting Flight Commander** — cadre officers (typically Major or
  Captain), the Assistant Professors of Aerospace Studies (APAS).
- **Noncommissioned Officer in Charge (NCOIC) / Unit Admissions NCO** — senior enlisted cadre.
- **Cadet Wing Commander (CW/CC)** — the senior cadet at the detachment; commands the cadet wing.
- **Cadet Vice Wing Commander / Cadet Deputy** and the **Cadet Wing staff** (A-staff sections).
- **Cadet Group Commander** — only at larger detachments with enough cadets to warrant a group echelon.
- **Cadet Squadron Commander** — commands a cadet squadron.
- **Cadet Flight Commander** — commands a flight; typically a POC (Professional Officer Course) cadet.
- **Cadet Flight Sergeant** — the senior enlisted-equivalent leader of the flight, directly responsible
  for the GMC (General Military Course) cadets in that flight.
- **The individual cadet.**

**Practical point for the test:** for the "down to a cadet" portion, most instructors want the national
chain memorized exactly and the local chain by *position*, with the current names filled in from your
own detachment. Ask your cadre for your det's current Det/CC and Cadet Wing Commander — those are not
findable from public sources and are not included here.

**Regional level:** AFROTC historically used four geographic regions (Northwest, Southwest, Southeast,
Northeast) between HQ AFROTC and the detachments. **⚠️ I could NOT confirm whether the region echelon is
still active in 2026** — every source I found describing regions was several years old. Ask your cadre
directly whether your detachment reports through a region. Do not assert a region in your answer unless
your cadre confirms it.

## ⚠️ FLAGS — Read These Carefully

### FLAG 1 — HIGH SEVERITY: Chief of Space Operations changed TODAY
Gen. Douglas A. Schiess assumed the office at a ceremony on **3 September 2026**, the very date of this
research. **Every printed reference, including the June 2026 Almanac the detachment supplied, still lists
Gen. B. Chance Saltzman.** The correct current answer is **Schiess**. Expect your instructor's slides to
say Saltzman. Be ready to answer either way and to explain the 3 Sep transition.

### FLAG 2 — HIGH SEVERITY: The Holm Center is NOT under Air University anymore
This is the single most likely thing to catch a cadet out, because nearly all AFROTC study material still
teaches the old chain.

- **Old (pre-Oct 2024):** AETC → **Air University** → Holm Center → AFROTC
- **Current:** AETC → **Air Force Recruiting Service / Air Force Accessions Center** → Holm Center → AFROTC

On **8 October 2024** the Holm Center was formally relieved from assignment to Air University and
reassigned to Air Force Recruiting Service, attached to the Air Force Accessions Center. The transition
completed in stages through **October 2025**. The Holm Center's own web presence now lives at
`afaccessionscenter.af.mil/Holm-Center/AFROTC/`, not on the Air University site.

**Complication:** the Air University website *still* hosts a Holm Center page
(`airuniversity.af.edu/Holm-Center/`), which is legacy content and reinforces the outdated answer.

**How to handle it on a test:** If asked to list the AFROTC chain, give the current structure. If your
detachment's official material still teaches the Air University version, **give the answer your cadre
teaches and note that the Holm Center was reassigned to AFRS/AFAC in October 2024.** Do not argue; do
demonstrate that you know both. Air University is still a real AETC organization — Lt. Gen. Daniel H.
Tulley is its Commander and President (took command November 2025, Maxwell AFB, AL) — it is simply no
longer AFROTC's parent.

### FLAG 3 — MEDIUM: "Department of War" is not statutory
Do not say the department was "renamed." It was **not**. EO 14347 (5 Sep 2025) authorized a **secondary
title**. The FY2026 NDAA did not codify it. Congressional action was still pending as of 3 Sep 2026.
Statutory titles remain **Department of Defense** and **Secretary of Defense**. See section 2 above for
the exact phrasing to use.

### FLAG 4 — MEDIUM: Two AFROTC-chain names changed within the last 6 weeks
- **Holm Center/CC:** Brig. Gen. Christopher T. Lay, only since **18 August 2026** (16 days ago).
  Predecessor Brig. Gen. Joseph L. Sheffield appears in most current-looking sources.
  Also note: an af.mil biography page for Brig. Gen. Christopher T. Lay still lists him as Commander,
  Force Generation Center, AFRC, Robins AFB — **that bio is stale.** The DVIDS ceremony imagery from
  18 Aug 2026 is authoritative.
- **HQ AFROTC/CC:** Col. Michael G. Fleming, only since **29 June 2026**.
  I could not identify the outgoing AFROTC commander by name from public sources.

### FLAG 5 — MEDIUM: Maj. Gen. Nelson's rank is in flux across sources
His official afaccessionscenter.af.mil biography page is titled "**Brigadier** General Jeffrey W. Nelson"
and he was styled Brig. Gen. as recently as April 2026, but DVIDS captions from the 18 August 2026 Holm
Center ceremony style him "**Maj. Gen.**" I am treating **Major General** as current, since the more
recent source wins, but the official bio page has not been updated. **If the test asks for his rank,
Major General is most likely correct; flag the discrepancy if challenged.**

### FLAG 6 — LOW/MEDIUM: CMSSF Bentivegna not re-verified after the CSO transition
CMSSF John F. Bentivegna has served since 15 September 2023 — approaching three years, a natural rotation
point, and senior enlisted advisors frequently turn over near a service chief transition. The most recent
confirmations I have are the June 2026 Almanac and a Wikipedia revision dated 31 July 2026, both showing
him incumbent. **I found no announcement of a successor, but I could not verify past 31 July 2026.**
Verify with your cadre if CMSSF is on the test.

### FLAG 7 — LOW: Vice Chief of Space Operations
Gen. Shawn N. Bratton is listed by the June 2026 Almanac. Not independently re-verified, and vice-chief
positions often shift with a new service chief. Low risk (rarely tested), but do not state it with
confidence.

### FLAG 8 — LOW: AFROTC region echelon unconfirmed
See "Regional level" above. Could not confirm whether the four AFROTC regions are still active. Ask cadre.

### FLAG 9 — INFORMATIONAL: The AETC → "Airman Development Command" rename did NOT happen
In February 2024 the Air Force announced it would redesignate AETC as **Airman Development Command (ADC)**,
originally targeted for 1 October 2025. **Those plans were put on hold in January 2025 and the change now
appears unlikely to proceed.** **The correct answer is still "Air Education and Training Command (AETC)."**
If a classmate or an old slide says "Airman Development Command," that is wrong.

### FLAG 10 — METHODOLOGY: af.mil and airuniversity.af.edu blocked direct fetching
Direct page fetches to `af.mil`, `airuniversity.af.edu`, `jcs.mil`, `jbsa.mil`, and
`afaccessionscenter.af.mil` returned **HTTP 403 Forbidden** to my tooling. Those sources were therefore
verified **indirectly** — through search-engine indexing of those same .mil pages, plus directly-fetched
DVIDS (`dvidshub.net`) and Air & Space Forces Magazine content. **Every name in this document traces to
a .mil page or a DVIDS official caption**, but a cadet with a browser should spot-check the two highest-risk
items (CSO Schiess and Holm Center/CC Lay) on the official sites directly.
