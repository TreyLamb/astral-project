Heads-up: this is the only official rulebook I could find, and it is from 2010. Detachments often run a local version. If your cadre's brief contradicts something here, cadre wins on the day. Use this to learn the game, not to argue a call.

The Game in 60 Seconds

Icarus is dodgeball built as an air campaign. Two teams of 10–15 each defend 13 target cones and attack the other team's. You don't just grab a ball and run. A player called the AOC Director sends you out on one mission at a time. You touch a comm station, pick up one ball of the color your mission uses, make one throw, leave by the nearest sideline, and go to the back of the line.

You win a round by achieving air superiority, which means knocking out one of three things: all 3 enemy AOC cones, both enemy bases, or all 3 enemy comm stations. Then the clock stops, points are scored, the field resets and the graveyard empties.

The point of the exercise (para 1): "achieve air superiority, suppress the enemy's ability to do the same, and inflict damage on the enemy's ability to wage aerial warfare by simulating Air and Space Power Functions." It was also used as a leadership game at Airman Leadership School, to teach "air and space principles along with the fundamentals of leadership, followership and teamwork." (Spangdahlem AB, 2010.)

The Battle Area

  • Field: 160 ft long × 90 ft wide (para 3). The center line is the FEBA, the Forward Edge of the Battle Area, 80 ft from each back line. Your half is friendly territory and the other half is enemy territory.
  • White borders count as inside for SAM sites, bases and the DCA CAP (para 3). Standing on the line of your SAM site counts as being in it.
  • Equipment: 26 target cones (13 per side) and 24 rubber balls, called ordnance (8.5 in., 12 per side).
  • Graveyards (para 11) sit beside the field. Yours is on the opposite end from your AOC, which is why the diagram labels both of them "Enemy Graveyard". You stay there until the field resets.

What the tables mean:

  • Only OCA and DCA players can be hit. SAM players, loggies and the AOC Director are never legitimate targets. Hitting one of them sends the thrower to the graveyard (10.1.2.2).
  • Neither DCA nor SAM can throw across the FEBA. Doing it at a player who isn't a legitimate target is a LOAC violation (10.1.2.3).
  • OCA vs DCA: an OCA player can throw at the defending DCA, and the DCA can throw back. They are the only two roles that can hit each other.

How a Mission Works, Step by Step

  1. Line up. The whole team stands single file at the front of the AOC, facing midfield (para 7). The Director tasks people from the front of the line only. No cutting, swapping or saving spots (5.1).
  2. Get tasked. A legal tasking is a function and/or a direction (para 8), for example "OCA, left base", "SAM, center" or "Loggie". Leaving without a tasking, or from anywhere except the front of the line, is AWOL, and AWOL means the graveyard (10.1.4).
  3. Touch an active comm station. This is required before every mission (6.3, 8.1). Running toward the FEBA past the plane of the center comm station without touching one sends you back to the AOC (10.2.1.1). If the station you planned to touch is down, use another one.
  4. Pick up your ball at the base you were sent to (OCA/DCA), or carry your yellow ball from the AOC (SAM). Picking up a different color than your mission uses, like taking green when you were tasked OCA, is AWOL (10.1.4.4).
  5. Do the mission.
    • OCA: cross the FEBA. Once you're across, you can't move backward until you've thrown (8.5). Sideways is fine. If you decide not to throw, leave by the nearest boundary.
    • DCA: walk straight from the base into the CAP (8.1) and wait for an enemy OCA to come into your half.
    • SAM: stand inside your site. If the Director sent you to a site that's already occupied, one of you has to leave (8.2).
  6. Throw once. A ball counts as thrown the moment it leaves your hand (9.3). Aim below the neck (9.2).
  7. Leave the field by the nearest boundary and go straight to the AOC (8.3), to the back of the line (5.1). Once you're off the field, the only way back on is through the AOC (8.4).

If you get hit while holding a ball: walk off at the nearest boundary, drop it on the ground and go to the graveyard (8.6). Don't toss it toward a loggie or back toward your side. That gets you ejected for unsportsmanlike conduct (10.1.1.2).

Rules of Engagement

  • Hits count below the neck (9.2).
  • A cone is destroyed only by a thrown ball, of any color (9.5). Knocking one over with your hand or foot doesn't count, and an OCA player who knocks a cone down without throwing is sent to the graveyard. In the rulebook's words, "AF does not support kamikaze missions" (10.1.3.7).
  • Only officials stand cones back up (9.4). A cone that was knocked down illegally can still be hit and "destroyed" while the ref is walking over to reset it.
  • One throw can count twice. If a single ball hits more than one legitimate target, every one of them is struck (6.1, para 12). There's no friendly fire on players ("No Fratricide", para 12). If you accidentally knock down your own cone, it stays down (9.7).
  • Only loggies pick up loose balls (9.6). Anyone else who catches, stops or deflects a thrown ball goes to the graveyard (10.1.3.5). Let loose balls roll.
  • Hitting enemy cones after you've been called out (hit, violation or mission complete) is unsportsmanlike: possible ejection, and the cone is reset (9.7).

Ejection-level offenses:

  • Automatic ejection: throwing your ball toward your own side after you've been killed or finished your mission (10.1.1.2, 10.2.2.2), and a flagrant headshot, which gets you ejected for the entire game (10.1.2.1).
  • Can get you ejected: hitting cones after you've been called out (9.7), and unsportsmanlike conduct, quibbling, inappropriate language or questioning an official's judgment (10.3). For these, the Head Official chooses between the graveyard and ejection.

Loggies: The Rules Nobody Reads

They're two players in blue mesh jerseys (6.4). The game runs on them, because nobody else is allowed to touch a loose ball.

  • Never a target. You can go anywhere on the field, in both halves (8.7).
  • No more than 2 balls at a time. Carry them, never roll or kick them. The one exception: if you've set a ball just outside a live base while restocking a dead one, you can roll it into the base as long as your hand stays on it (10.1.3.3).
  • Set balls on the ground inside the base or AOC. Never hand one to a player: both of you go to the graveyard (10.1.3.9).
  • No more than 2 red + 2 green in a base (6.4).
  • A dead base gets restocked before a live one (5.2, 6.4). Getting this wrong is an ROE violation (back of the line).
  • Don't hang around in enemy territory. "Loitering" means more than 3 seconds inside the same 25 sq ft (para 17). Don't screen or block, whether for your own players or against the enemy's (8.7).
  • A loggie sent to the graveyard isn't replaced and keeps the jersey (6.4). Your team plays short a loggie until the reset.
  • A loggie who commits an ROE violation goes to the back of the line, and the balls they were carrying go to midfield (6.4).

Clock, Start, Stops and Scoring

Teams (para 2): 10–15 players per team, and team sizes can differ by no more than 1. Team sizes are checked at every air superiority and at halftime, and when a team has to sit someone, the AOC Director chooses who. Substitutions to even out team sizes happen only at halftime. Everyone plays at least one half of every game, and nobody sits a second half until everyone in the flight has sat once. No duplicate jersey numbers.

Jerseys (para 16): a clearly displayed whole number, no more than two digits, on either the issued FLA jersey or the flight T-shirt. If your number duplicates someone else's or your shirt doesn't follow the rule, you don't play.

Start (para 7): everyone lines up single file from the front of the AOC. Each team deploys up to 7 players onto the field, to SAM sites and bases only, plus 2 loggies outside the boundaries. Everyone else stays in line.

Time (para 4): two 5-minute periods with a 2-minute halftime, or 30 minutes total, whichever comes first. Teams switch sides at the half. The clock stops only for:

  • air superiority
  • multiple or major rule violations
  • safety
  • an officials' timeout

The AOC Director can call "Time remaining" to get a time check.

Whistle (para 13): when any official blows a whistle, stop where you are and take a knee. Play resumes on the Head Official's whistle.

Officials (para 14): at least 4. The Head Official keeps time, starts and stops play, makes final rulings and resets the field after air superiority.

Scoring (para 15)

EventPoints
Achieving air superiority3

  • When a team gets air superiority: the clock stops, points are awarded, the field resets, the graveyard empties, and team sizes are rebalanced.
  • When a period ends without air superiority: each team still gets 1 point per enemy base and comm station destroyed (15.5). Partial damage to the AOC is worth nothing.
  • Example: you take out all 3 comm stations and 1 base. That's air superiority 3 + comm stations 3 + base 1 = 7 points. If you'd taken out the AOC instead, it would be 3 + 5 = 8, plus anything else already destroyed.

What the Rules Imply

These are my own conclusions from the rules above. They aren't in the rulebook and are not from cadre.

  • Comm stations are the cheapest way to win. Each one is a single cone, each is worth a point even if you don't finish the job, and taking out all three gives you air superiority. Every station you remove also gives the enemy one fewer route to their missions. Check the diagram, though: the center station sits inside the enemy DCA CAP, so it's the defended one. The two back-corner stations aren't.
  • Losing SAM sites doesn't lose you anything directly. A SAM site with nobody in it is just a cone, and SAM players can't be hit, so the Director should keep the sites manned. That makes a SAM tasking the safest job on the field.
  • Your own cones are your own problem. Accidentally knocking one over keeps it down (9.7), and so does blocking one, so don't hang around near your own cones.
  • Line discipline is the whole game for most players. For any one player, most violations come down to doing something out of order: leaving from the wrong spot, skipping the comm station, retreating after crossing the FEBA, or touching a loose ball. Being slow costs time. Being out of order costs the game.
  • The Director is effectively your last line of defense. With a yellow ball in hand, the Director can shoot an inbound OCA from inside the AOC (5.1). Keep a yellow ball at the AOC.

Quick Checklist Before You Play

  • Tasked from the front of the line?
  • Touched an active comm station?
  • Picked up the right color for the mission you were given?
  • OCA: no backing up after you cross the FEBA. DCA: stay in the CAP. SAM: stay inside the site.
  • Aimed below the neck?
  • Left by the nearest boundary and went to the back of the line?
  • Hit? Drop the ball, don't toss it.
  • Not a loggie? Don't touch loose balls.
  • Whistle? Take a knee.

Gaps and Oddities in the Rulebook

  • The game length is ambiguous. Two 5-minute periods plus a 2-minute half is 12 minutes, yet the same sentence says "or 30 total minutes; whichever occurs first." The likely reading is that the 30 minutes is a real-time cap that includes stoppages, but the book doesn't say. Ask cadre.
  • A reference that doesn't exist. Rule 6.1 cites "rules 9.5 and 10.11". There is no 10.11. It probably means 10.1.1 (Good Kill).
  • Numbering mistakes: there are two paragraphs numbered 10.1.4 (written "10.1.4. 3"), and the table of contents says "Functions/Roles" while the body says "Functions". These are cosmetic, but don't quote paragraph numbers to an official with too much confidence.
  • No tiebreaker. The book scores points and never says what happens if a game ends tied.
  • No penalty listed for a SAM player leaving the site early. The rule just says they "must remain within the SAM's boundary to fire". Throwing from outside the site is AWOL (10.1.4.3).
  • "FLA" is never defined in the book. It's the issued jersey.
  • The book is from 2010. I found no newer version in public sources. Your detachment's local brief overrides this one.
